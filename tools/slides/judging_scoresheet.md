# How the rubric works

## What this workshop settles

Every simulation this autumn is scored on **the official ABCC rubric** — the
same sheet the real judges hold in Winnipeg.

- What the **seven categories** are, and how they are weighted
- What judges ask in each — and what a **top-box answer** looks like
- How the sheet runs **our own mocks**: clock, feedback, external judges

::: notes
Source document: 01-competition/judging_scoresheet.md — the printable rubric
replica used in every HSBI simulation. Baseline: 2025 Event Guide, applied to
all rounds. Re-verify against the 2027 Competition Guide when it lands.
:::

## The mechanics

- **Seven categories**, each scored **1–10 in whole numbers**
- Score × weight, summed — a **weighted total out of 105** (weights sum to 10.5)
- The **same rubric applies to all rounds**
- The criterion questions are **official wording**; the 3/7/10 anchors are
  our coaching interpretation, so scores stay comparable across judges and
  months

## The seven categories and their weights

| # | Category | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Implementation & Resources | **×2.5** |
| 2 | Analysis | ×2.0 |
| 3 | Recommendation | ×2.0 |
| 4 | Communicating Effectively | ×1.0 |
| 5 | Framing the Problem | ×1.0 |
| 6 | Impact | ×1.0 |
| 7 | Measurement, Risks & Mitigation | ×1.0 |

What each judge asks per category follows — one slide each.

## The heavy categories matter twice

Ties are not coin flips.

- First tie-breaker: **the traditional case**
- Further ties: **rubric-category performance in weight order**

So Implementation & Resources, Analysis, and Recommendation decide the score
— and then decide the tie as well. **Together they carry 6.5 of the 10.5
weight points.**

# The heavy three

## 1 · Implementation & Resources — ×2.5

**The single heaviest category.** The official question:

> Is there a clear path to follow, with resources, time, and financial
> considerations?

The external-judge briefing compresses it into one habit —
**ask who does what, by when, paid from where.**

## Implementation — what the anchors say

- **3** — one generic "Phase 1/2/3" slide; no owners, no budget, no timeline;
  everything asserted, not shown
- **7** — credible phased roadmap with timeline and cost estimates; some
  figures round guesses, funding source or sequencing thin
- **10** — a path the client could **start Monday**: dated milestones, named
  owners and headcount, itemized budget reconciled with case financials,
  funding source, explicit time considerations

## 2 · Analysis — ×2.0

Is the assessment substantiated by **research, logic, and quantitative +
qualitative analysis**?

- **3** — framework recital; case numbers restated, not used; claims
  unsupported
- **7** — sound logic, relevant quant work (sizing, break-even), qualitative
  insight; an assumption unchecked or a data point ignored
- **10** — research, logic, quant + qual all **converge on the
  recommendation**; assumptions stated and stress-tested; every claim
  traceable

## 3 · Recommendation — ×2.0

Is it **effective, clear, comprehensive, and a strategic fit**?

- **3** — vague or generic ("improve marketing"); does not follow from the
  analysis or fit this client
- **7** — clear, actionable, plausible fit; prioritisation thin, alternatives
  dismissed too quickly
- **10** — answers the client's **actual question**; fit with mandate,
  constraints, and capabilities made explicit; alternatives weighed and
  rejected for stated reasons

# The four ×1.0 categories

## 4 · Communicating Effectively

Clear, professional, compelling presentation — and a readable deck?

- **3** — reading from a cluttered deck; uneven airtime; timing missed; Q&A
  rambles
- **7** — professional and clear; action titles; confident answer-first Q&A;
  energy occasionally flat
- **10** — a story told **to the judges, not the screen**; instantly readable
  deck; all four speakers strong; composed Q&A; finishes on time

## 5 · Framing the Problem

Are the issues wrapped into a clear, solvable summary — primary + secondary?

- **3** — problem restated verbatim, or misidentified; no primary/secondary
  distinction
- **7** — clear, solvable statement with the primary issue identified;
  secondary issues loosely connected
- **10** — sharp synthesis that **sets up the entire deck**; scope and
  success criteria explicit

## 6 · Impact

Quantitative and qualitative benefit to the organization?

- **3** — benefits asserted ("this will increase revenue") — no numbers, no
  logic
- **7** — quantified upside with stated assumptions; sizing partly rough or
  unconnected to the cost side
- **10** — credible quantified benefit (revenue, cost, or mission KPI) from
  defensible assumptions, plus qualitative benefits tied to the
  organisation's goals — **visibly worth the implementation cost**

## 7 · Measurement, Risks & Mitigation

Progress measurement and realistic risk mitigation?

- **3** — no KPIs; risks absent, or a token list without mitigations
- **7** — sensible KPIs, top risks each paired with a mitigation; some
  generic; no targets or owners
- **10** — concrete KPIs with **targets, review cadence, and owners**;
  material risks with specific mitigations and a trigger-based contingency
  where it matters

# The sheet in our simulations

## One sheet per judge, every mock

- Print **one sheet per judge per team** — header: Date · Case · Format ·
  Prep time · Judge · Team
- The **same seven questions** in every simulation, exactly as written
- **External judges** at W17, W23–24, and both W25 rounds — and W19 is graded
  too, if a judge is available

## The rules of the room — all formats

- Prep time enforced **exactly**; phones and smartwatches surrendered;
  **one laptop per student**
- Internet + GenAI allowed · **no coach contact after release**
- Deck collected at the fixed pickup ("USB") — late arrival earns no extra
  time
- Any mention of HSBI in front of a judge is flagged — real competition:
  **disqualification**
- **Yellow card at 5 minutes remaining, red at 1** — never extended

## Round timing at a glance

| Round | Weight | Prep | Present | Q&A |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepared | 25 % | at home | 25 min | 15 min |
| Boardroom | 30 % | 4 h | 3-min pitch, protected | 22-min discussion |
| Traditional → 10-hour case (2027) | 45 % | 3 h → **10 h** | 20 min | 10 min |
| Final | — | deck frozen | 20 min | 15 min |

Boardroom: one summary slide only in the pitch, appendices blocked — then
accessible in the discussion. **Every member speaks** in every round.

## Feedback — within 15 minutes

- **Two strengths**, each named by category
- **Two gaps** — prioritise the **×2.5/×2.0** categories
- **One drill** to close the biggest gap, with its source in `04-training/`
- **One improvement goal per person** — transferred verbatim into
  `02-plan/simulation_log.md`

## Briefing an external judge

- **Play the client's decision-maker**, not a teacher — the person who would
  fund and own this
- Seven categories, whole numbers 1–10, the 3/7/10 anchors
- **Implementation counts 2.5×** — who does what, by when, paid from where
- Practitioner questions: challenge numbers and feasibility; interrupt
  unevidenced claims; make every member speak
- The clock is hard · blunt is a gift
- Afterwards: scores into `02-plan/simulation_log.md` within 24 h, and a
  thank-you

## The one-line takeaway

**Score the weights, not the vibe.**

6.5 of 10.5 weight points sit in Implementation & Resources, Analysis, and
Recommendation — build and rehearse the deck in that order, and read every
mock score against it.
