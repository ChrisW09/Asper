# The two questions

## What this briefing settles

Two questions come up in every kick-off, and both have a documented answer.

- **May the team use AI during the competition?**
- **What may the professor/coach do — and not do?**

Everything here traces to the official **ABCC 2025 Event Guide**, the fullest
published rulebook, plus the 2027 website.

# 1 · May we use AI?

## Yes — explicitly, and in writing

The rules allow internet use during timed prep, including

> "any login-required website, including password-protected databases from
> their University, filesharing websites, **and Generative AI**."

That covers **the 4-hour boardroom prep and the third-round prep** — 3 h in
2025/2026, a **10-hour case** in 2027 (assumed to carry over; we have asked).
In the prepared case, worked on at home, all resources are allowed anyway.

## Every team has the same arsenal

So access is not the edge. **A drilled procedure is.**

- **One designated operator** — the CS & analytics lead, not four people prompting
- **Narrow sanctioned tasks** — checking math, stress-testing the recommendation,
  generating counterarguments, polishing brief wording, the 10-minute
  Q&A-anticipation routine
- **The hard rule:** no unverified number reaches a slide

## What is never outsourced

Framing · option choice · the recommendation.

Judges interrogate the team, not the tool. **Borrowed logic collapses in the
22-minute discussion** — that is the round where a thin answer is exposed, and
it is worth 30 % of the score.

## Two limits that still bind

- **Confidentiality.** The cases are confidential competition documents
  ("no reproduction or transmission"). Do not paste full case text into consumer
  AI tools — prefer university-licensed enterprise tools and work from your own
  summaries.
- **Devices.** One laptop per student. No phones, no smartwatches. Volunteers
  check the prep rooms.

Check the 2027 guide for new AI clauses before the competition.

# 2 · What may the coach do?

## Fully in the game — three windows

| Window | What the coach may do |
| --- | --- |
| **Prepared case** (release → submission) | Unrestricted. "Teams may use all resources at their disposal," explicitly including faculty input: review storylines, challenge the model, run rehearsals |
| **Before the competition** | Everything in this repository — training, simulations, feedback, selection, logistics, registration, all organizer contact |
| **On site** | Attend presentations, sit in on the judges' feedback sessions, work the entire social programme |

Only sponsor-employee contact is banned — for everyone.

## Silent — three moments

- **From case release to presentation** (boardroom and round 3): *no contact
  with the team whatsoever* — in 2027 that is **all of Fri Feb 19 and Sat
  morning** until the team has presented, meals and evening included.
- **During any presentation or Q&A:** any communication between coach and team,
  or any coach intervention in the question period, means
  **immediate disqualification**.
- **In the final:** advisors of finalist teams may not communicate with their
  team until after it has presented.

Excursion day (Thu Feb 18) is not a round — contact is fine, anonymity is not
suspended.

## Anonymity binds the coach too

Nothing said to a judge, worn, or shown may identify HSBI.

- The team competes under **a fictional consulting name it chooses itself** and
  registers before the event
- Plus **a division letter A–I** drawn at on-site registration, which sets the
  week's presentation slots
- The name is ours; the letter is theirs
- Identifying your school to a judge = **disqualification**

# The rule of thumb

## Influence ends at case release

The coach's influence ends at case release and resumes at the feedback session.

**Everything the team needs from you must already be in their habits by then.**

That is what the autumn simulation calendar is for —
