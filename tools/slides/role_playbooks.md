# One team, four owners

## What this briefing settles

Roles are assigned today. Three things to walk out with:

- **The four roles** — and the artifacts each one owns, round by round
- **The shared clock** — six phases that run identically in the 3-hour,
  4-hour and **10-hour** preps, so hand-offs are predictable
- **The GenAI SOP** — one designated operator, narrow sanctioned tasks, and
  the hard rule: **no unverified number reaches a slide**

2027 runs **Mon Feb 15 – Sat Feb 20**; the third round is a **10-hour case**
(working assumption — confirm with the organizers).

## The rounds these roles serve

| Round | Weight | Format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prepared** | 25 % | 25 + 15 · .pptx by e-mail · late = bottom of pool |
| **Boardroom** | 30 % | 4-h prep · protected 3 min · 22-min discussion |
| **10-hour case** | 45 % | Fri prep · Sat 20 + 10 — **where the competition is won** |
| **Final** | top 3 | frozen decks · 20 + 15 · ties break on round 3 |

Every member speaks in every round. Judges get our **one-page black-and-white
brief** before the boardroom discussion.

# The shared team clock

## Six phases, every timed round

The clock starts the second the case is handed over — **late arrival earns no
extra time**.

| Phase | 3 h | 4 h | 10 h | Gate output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 Read & frame | 0:00–0:20 | 0:00–0:25 | 0:00–0:45 | All read · facts logged |
| P2 Align & decide | 0:20–0:40 | 0:25–0:55 | 0:45–1:30 | Rec locked (10 h: provisional) |
| P3 Analysis | 0:40–1:40 | 0:55–2:15 | 1:30–6:00 | Model · evidence · concept |
| P4 Build | 1:40–2:25 | 2:15–3:15 | 6:00–8:00 | Deck (+ brief) in template |
| P5 Verify & rehearse | 2:25–2:50 | 3:15–3:45 | 8:00–9:30 | Audit · Q&A grid · run-through |
| P6 Lockdown & USB | 2:50–3:00 | 3:45–4:00 | 9:30–9:45 | Files named · saved · verified |

## Two hard gates

- **P2 direction lock.** The chosen direction is reopened only by the
  strategist — at an announced **10-minute cost**.
- **P6 number freeze.** After it, nothing new goes on a slide — no exceptions.

The prepared case has no room clock, but the same ownership applies: every
artifact below has exactly one owner in that round too.

# The 10-hour day

## Ten hours, two gates, one lunch

| Clock | Block |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–1:30 | Read & frame · provisional rec · **written research plan** |
| 1:30–3:30 | Research & first analysis — GenAI lane, every figure verified |
| 3:30 | **Gate A** — direction confirmed, paper storyboard |
| 3:50–4:20 | **Lunch** — all four, away from screens |
| 4:20–6:00 | Deep analysis — full model, implementation plan, KPIs |
| 6:00 | **Gate B** — storyline frozen, every action title written |
| 6:00–8:00 | Build — rolling merge; break with food ~7:00 |
| 8:30 | **Content and number freeze** |
| 8:30–9:45 | Full 20 + 10 rehearsal · AI Q&A routine · USB |

## What changes in ten hours

- **The bar rises.** Judges expect real research, a tested model and a
  first-90-days plan — not a slower three-hour deck
- **The risk changes.** From running out of time to running out of
  energy: lunch is non-negotiable; the CS lead calls the breaks
- **Gate A is the one free re-decision.** After it, reopening costs 10 minutes
- **No deck work overnight** — dinner, one read of the Q&A grid, sleep
- **The 3-hour clock stays drilled** — it is the fallback if the organizers
  confirm a shorter round

## The 10-hour day by role

| Role | What the extra seven hours buy |
| --- | --- |
| **Finance** | Full model by 6:00 — scenarios, sensitivity, funding; deep appendix bank |
| **Innovation** | Confirm-or-kill evidence for the idea by Gate A; pilot design |
| **CS & analytics** | Runs the research plan; source log; calls the breaks |
| **Strategist** | Owns the research plan, both gates and the 8:30 freeze |

# 1 · Finance lead

## Own every number

Every number the team shows **or says**. The heaviest rubric line,
*Implementation & resources* (2.5×), asks for financial considerations;
*Analysis* (2.0×) demands quantitative substantiation. Nothing financial
reaches a slide unless this role produced or verified it.

- **Prepared:** full model workbook · impact exhibit · costed budget · sensitivity
- **Boardroom:** the three headline figures · brief's financial lines · appendix
- **Round 3 (10 h):** impact + budget slides · assumptions register
- **Final:** nothing new — total command of every number for Q&A

## Finance — minute-map

| Phase | 3 h (speed runs) | Boardroom adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Exhibits first; every figure to the register | +5 min on baseline economics |
| P2 | 2-min "size of the prize" per option | Flag what is costable in 4 h |
| P3 | Model from prebuilt shell; push interim outputs | Sensitivity + funding view |
| P4 | Model → 2–3 slides; exec-summary numbers | 3 headline figures · brief lines |
| P5 | **Number audit** · AI math-check · brief the team | "Walk me through your model" |
| P6 | Freeze register; tie deck to workbook | Brief readable in b/w print |

## Finance — failure modes

- **Over-modeling** — the model becomes an end in itself → slide-ready outputs
  by end of P3; the strategist may call "good enough" and it stands
- **Orphan numbers** — slides drift from the model → one workbook is the single
  source of truth; final tie-out at the P6 freeze
- **False precision** — "€1,247,332" invites attack → round aggressively, show
  ranges, label assumptions
- **Freezing on a challenge** → the register carries rationale + sensitivity
  per input: "at half our uptake, payback moves 18 → 30 months — the
  recommendation holds"

# 2 · Innovation lead

## Own the distinctiveness

*Recommendation* weighs 2.0× — and **nine teams see the same case**. Recent
clients explicitly wanted boldness (Jazz Winnipeg: a "radical new operational
idea"; Coal & Canary: premium to luxury; Access Credit Union: youth-engaging
innovation).

**Generate wide · kill fast · make the survivor vivid and implementable.**

- **Prepared:** option tree with kill log · concept slides · pilot design
- **Boardroom:** the rec's core wording on the summary slide · counterargument sheet
- **Round 3 (10 h):** concept + "why this wins" · alternatives-considered slide
- **Final:** sharpened articulation · every "why not X?" rehearsed

## Innovation — minute-map

| Phase | 3 h (speed runs) | Boardroom adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Read for customer/market signals; 5–7 option stubs | What did the client try before? |
| P2 | 30-second pitches; run the kill; lock direction | Draft the one-sentence rec |
| P3 | Make it concrete: features, journey, differentiation | Pilot + "day one" experience |
| P4 | Concept + alternatives slides (scored under *Analysis*) | Own the rec wording · appendix |
| P5 | Red-team own idea via the AI counterargument lane | Tune for the 22-min discussion |
| P6 | Coherence read: every slide serves the story | Same |

## Innovation — failure modes

- **Falling in love with idea #1** → minimum five options and a **written kill
  log** before the P2 lock — no exceptions
- **Clever but unimplementable** — implementation is the 2.5× line → every
  concept element maps to a resourced step, or it is cut / parked as "phase 2"
- **Reopening the decision mid-build** → only the strategist can, at the
  10-minute cost
- **A generic answer eight other teams will give** → P3 check: "Which two teams
  will say exactly this — and what is our twist?"

# 3 · CS & analytics lead

## Own the evidence and the machinery

Fastest researcher, data wrangler, chart builder, deck mechanic — and the
team's **designated AI operator**. *Analysis* (2.0×) rewards research rigor.
Also owns **file compliance** — .pptx only, exact file names, USB handover at
the fixed pickup time — so nobody else thinks about mechanics.

- **Prepared:** research pack + source log · master deck · the submission e-mail
- **Boardroom:** numbered **appendix map** — any judge question answered in seconds
- **Round 3 (10 h):** benchmark table with sources · exhibits · USB save
- **Final:** printed Q&A grid and appendix map (deck edits impossible)

## CS & analytics — minute-map

| Phase | 3 h (speed runs) | Boardroom adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Workspace up: template, timer; exhibit data to sheet | Same |
| P2 | Rapid benchmark scan; open the source log | Same |
| P3 | Timeboxed research sprints; charts; run the AI lane | One deeper data pull |
| P4 | **Rolling deck merge**; number the appendices | Lay out the b/w brief · print-test |
| P5 | Compliance checklist; 10-min AI Q&A routine | Rehearse driving the appendices |
| P6 | Save to USB, **open from the stick**, keep backup | Deck and brief both |

## CS & analytics — failure modes

- **Research rabbit holes** → every sprint timeboxed at 15 minutes; the
  strategist can call "park it"
- **Deck-merge crunch at the end** → rolling merge from the start of P4; slide
  owners deliver **inside the template**, never in loose files
- **An unsourced factoid reaches a slide** → the source log is mandatory;
  verification enforced at P5 — no exceptions for "I saw it somewhere"
- **Tech failure at handover** → save early, verify it opens, keep a second
  copy; never rename files after the compliance check

# 4 · General-business strategist

## Own the argument and the clock

Integrate the three specialists into **one storyline covering every rubric
row** — above all *Implementation & resources* (2.5×, the single heaviest
category). **Timekeeper during prep, marshal during Q&A.**

- **Prepared:** framing + storyboard · exec summary · roadmap · Q&A grid ·
  speaker allocation (every member must speak)
- **Boardroom:** the one-page brief (drafting owner) · protected-3-minute
  script · discussion battle plan
- **Round 3 (10 h):** framing · storyline · KPI/risk slide · timing plan 20 + 10
- **Final:** Q&A grid v2 · timing for 20 + 15, fresh judge panel

## Strategist — minute-map

| Phase | 3 h (speed runs) | Boardroom adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Read for the decision-maker's actual question | Same |
| P2 | Chair the vote; lock the rec; assign slides; post the clock | Sketch the brief skeleton |
| P3 | Implementation skeleton; halfway thesis check | Deeper resourcing + sequencing |
| P4 | Exec summary; sequence the story; KPI/risk; enforce gates | **Brief early** — then 3-min script |
| P5 | Run rehearsal; allocate Q&A themes; AI routine with CS | Drill the 3 min to **≤ 2:45** |
| P6 | Every rubric row has a home slide | Brief included |

## Strategist — failure modes

- **Storyline as afterthought** → a paper storyboard exists by the end of P2;
  slides fit the story, never the reverse
- **Thin implementation slide in the 2.5× category** → hard minimum: phases,
  owners, budget line from finance, timeline, first-90-days detail, KPIs
- **Running over time** → rehearse to 90 % of allotted time; when tight, the
  strategist **cuts content** — nobody speeds up delivery
- **Q&A chaos** → marshal protocol: each question routed to exactly one owner;
  others add only on a hand signal

# GenAI standard operating procedure

## One operator, three narrow lanes

Our stance: AI is **a fifth analyst with zero accountability** — fast,
occasionally wrong, never the one answering judges. It checks, stresses and
polishes; **it never does our thinking**.

Default: the **CS & analytics lead runs the team's AI lane** — never four chat
sessions producing conflicting output. Three personal lanes only:

- **Finance** — arithmetic verification of its own model outputs
- **Innovation** — counterarguments against the chosen concept, **P5 only**
- **Strategist** — wording polish in P4 · the Q&A routine in P5

## Sanctioned — and never outsourced

**Sanctioned:** checking math · stress-testing ("argue as a skeptical CFO") ·
counterarguments and likely judge questions · polishing exec-summary wording ·
condensing text for the brief · scouting benchmarks *for verification at
source* · formatting clean-up.

**Never outsourced:** the problem framing, the option choice, and the core
recommendation are made by the four of us. AI input is **one voice, not a
verdict** — any course change is a team decision the strategist chairs.

## No unverified number reaches a slide

1. Every external figure needs a **primary source** in the source log — an AI
   answer is never a source and is never cited
2. Finance independently recomputes any AI-checked math; on disagreement,
   finance's own calculation stands pending a source check
3. External benchmarks need **two independent sources** — or they are relabeled
   as assumptions with a stated basis
4. After the P6 number freeze: **no new AI-derived content** in deck, brief or
   talk track
5. Unverifiable in the time available → clearly labeled assumption, or **cut**

## Confidentiality cautions

The cases are confidential — no reproduction, sharing, or outside discussion.

- **Never paste** the case PDF, exhibits, or verbatim extracts into consumer AI
  tools — assume anything pasted may be retained
- Prefer **university-licensed tools** with no-training terms, login-tested
  before we fly
- Prompt by **paraphrase** — minimum necessary detail, never client-identifiable
  case language
- School name out of every prompt and screen; never use a tool to contact anyone
- Before February: read the 2027 guide for new AI clauses; ask the organizers

## The 10-minute Q&A routine

P5 · strategist + CS lead at one screen. **AI output never appears on a slide.**

| Minutes | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–2:00 | Strategist dictates a paraphrased brief — no case text pasted |
| 2:00–4:30 | Prompt 1: "15 hardest judge questions" — implementation first |
| 4:30–6:00 | Prompt 2: "Which three would this team struggle with most?" |
| 6:00–8:30 | Huddle: every question gets an owner + one-line answer; finance verifies |
| 8:30–10:00 | Script bridges for the two weakest spots; close the lane |

# The rule of thumb

## One clock, four owners

The clock is shared — the ownership is not. **Every artifact has exactly one
owner; every phase ends at a gate.**

- Direction locks at P2 · numbers freeze at P6
- One AI operator · sanctioned tasks only · no unverified number on a slide
- Every member speaks in every round
