# The first ten minutes

## The ask names the type

| The task sentence says | Type |
| --- | --- |
| viability · radical idea | **A** Turnaround |
| expansion · growth · pilot budget | **B** Growth & entry |
| positioning · brand elevation | **C** Premiumization |
| next product · opportunity, with numeric screens | **D** Opportunity screen |
| engage segment X · service innovation · digital | **E** Engagement |
| hiring · trust · adoption · change | **F** People & change |

Read the ask twice before anything else — the task sentence almost always
names the type.

## Then confirm against the exhibits

- A deficit P&L → **A**
- A market or competitor table → **B** or **D**
- Brand-tracking data → **C**
- Funnel or demographic data → **E**
- Workforce or adoption data → **F**

Where the types come from: the prepared round has been an arts/culture
non-profit three years running · the boardroom round an owner-led consumer or
family business at a crossroads · the traditional round a large Manitoba-rooted
enterprise with an engagement, trust or talent problem.

## Two rules for hybrids

- **The ask wins.** A bank case with brand-tracking exhibits but an ask about
  young customers is Type **E** — the brand data becomes evidence inside the E
  storyline.
- **Borrow slides, not storylines.** Take a single analysis slide from another
  type's template if an exhibit demands it, but never merge two storylines.
  A 20-minute deck carries exactly one narrative arc.

## Three constants, whatever the type

- **Implementation & Resources is 2.5×** — the roadmap slide is never the one
  you cut
- **Analysis is 2.0×** — every claim traces to an exhibit or a stated assumption
- **Measurement & risks is cheap insurance** — a risk table and a KPI strip take
  ten minutes and are pre-built in every template

Once the type is fixed, the five-minute selector in `04-training/frameworks.md`
gives the analysis plan — before anyone opens a spreadsheet.

# Type A · Turnaround

## Recognise it

*Precedents: WAG-Qaumajuq 2024, Manitoba Museum 2025, Jazz Winnipeg 2026 — all prepared round.*

A non-profit or arts organization · a structural deficit · flat or shrinking
grants · an aging audience · reserves with a countdown · an ask that says
"sustainable", "viable" or "radical new idea".

**Core question:** which *one* signature move closes the structural gap within
the organization's real capacity — without damaging the mission that legitimizes
its grants and sponsors?

## What decides it

1. A **deficit bridge** — which P&L lines leak, which could grow
2. A **revenue-mix benchmark** against two or three named peers who solved it
3. An **option screen**: financial impact × mission fit × execution capacity,
   with the rejected options shown
4. A **break-even model** of the signature move, with sensitivity

**Storyline:** burning platform → where the money leaks → what peers did →
option screen → the signature move in depth → the bridge to surplus →
stakeholder management → roadmap → risks and the ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Judge attacks:** "Your move dilutes the mission" → the mission-fit column ·
"A three-person team cannot execute this" → capacity column, phasing, partners ·
"What if demand doesn't come?" → sensitivity plus the pilot gate ·
"Why not just cut costs?" → cutting the programme cuts the reason funders pay.

**Traps:** the laundry list of ten unfunded ideas · recommending what the client
already tried · treating grants as guaranteed · forgetting that audience and
funders are different customers.

**Self-check:** can the treasurer photograph one slide with the full bridge from
−€X to +€Y?

# Type B · Growth & market entry

## Recognise it

*Precedent: Independent Jewellers 2024 (boardroom).*

An owner-led or family firm · a saturated or small home market · growth
ambition · often an explicit pilot budget · constraints such as brand-partner
rules or limited capital.

**Core question:** where to play and how to enter — and which single gated pilot
buys the most learning per euro before anything irreversible is committed?

## What decides it

1. A **market screen** over three to five candidates: attractiveness *and*
   right-to-win
2. A **transferable-advantage test** — what actually travels beyond the
   founder's home market
3. **Entry-mode economics** — own site vs digital-first vs partner vs pop-up
4. A **pilot design** with budget, KPIs and an explicit go/no-go gate

**Storyline:** why growth must come from outside → market screen → what travels
→ entry-mode comparison → unit economics and break-even → the gated pilot →
conditional phases → risks and a deliberately small ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Judge attacks:** "Why this market and not X?" → the screen answers it ·
"This cannibalizes your home store" → quantify the overlap · "Your marquee
constraint blocks this" → address it head-on · "What kills the pilot?" → state
the gate criteria before being asked.

**Traps:** size-only market ranking · "open stores" without unit economics ·
pretending the constraint away · a pilot without a kill criterion · asking for
the full rollout instead of the gate.

**Self-check:** is the ask small enough to say yes to inside 22 minutes?

# Type C · Premiumization

## Recognise it

*Precedent: Coal & Canary 2026 (boardroom).*

A consumer brand with craft or heritage credentials · a mid-tier price point ·
broad distribution · founders who want to move upmarket.

**Core question:** full repositioning or two-tier architecture — and what happens
to the existing channels and the cash engine while the brand climbs?

## What decides it

1. The **perception gap** — what the brand-tracking data grants permission for,
   and what it denies
2. The **luxury-codes checklist** — scarcity, materials, story, service — and
   which the company can credibly adopt
3. **Channel-consequence economics** — what pruning wholesale and exiting
   marketplaces costs in contribution
4. The **transition P&L**, with a haircut applied to the client's own plan

**Storyline:** permission evidence → codes we adopt (and refuse) → two-tier
architecture → channel hygiene with its price tag → margin-mix bridge → launch
sequence → roadmap → risks and the gated ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Judge attacks:** "You'll alienate the loyal core" → the core line stays, at its
price, for its customer · "Ubiquity already killed your luxury story" → the
pruning plan, with forgone contribution computed · "€95 candles from a €39
brand?" → pilot evidence: sell-out, waitlist, new-customer share.

**Traps:** betting the company on the top tier · luxury-by-price-increase alone ·
ignoring the voucher machinery that contradicts price integrity · scaling a
scarce line until it stops being scarce.

**Self-check:** does the deck state what you *refuse* to do?

# Type D · Opportunity screen

## Recognise it

*Precedent: AROW Global 2025 (boardroom).*

An industrial or B2B firm dominating a saturated niche · flat growth · an ask
with **explicit numeric screens**: revenue floor, margin floor, investment
ceiling, payback window, no-go list.

**Core question:** which *one* adjacent opportunity passes every stated screen —
and what is the cheapest staged validation before full commitment?

## What decides it

1. A **visible funnel** from longlist to one winner, with the criteria that cut
   at each stage
2. The **screen tested number by number** — every constraint restated and passed
3. A **capability-transfer map** — have / build / buy
4. A **gated validation plan** with a stated maximum sunk cost

**Storyline:** funnel → the constraint-compliance table (the centrepiece) → why
us → the market → validation gates and spend profile → roadmap → risks and a
gate-1-only ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Every attack here is arithmetic.** Judges recompute the compliance table live;
one number that fails under recomputation collapses the recommendation.
Second wave: "Why not the runner-up?" → funnel and appendix longlist ·
"The incumbent will respond" → fragmentation evidence, cost position ·
"Your margin assumption is optimistic" → bottom-up cost build in the appendix.

**Traps:** a menu instead of a winner · violating an explicit no-go · a market
sized only top-down · requesting the full investment with no gates · ignoring
the case's stated *preferences* — judges use them as tie-breakers.

**Self-check:** recompute every number in the compliance table out loud before
freeze. State the maximum loss if gate 1 kills the idea.

# Type E · Digital engagement

## Recognise it

*Precedent: Access Credit Union 2026 (traditional); People Corp 2025 shares elements.*

A large incumbent — bank, credit union, insurer, utility, benefits platform —
losing a demographic, usually the young, to digital-native rivals, with funnel
or demographic exhibits.

**Core question:** which segment-led offer bundle wins the target group back, at
an acquisition cost the lifetime value actually supports?

## What decides it

1. **Sub-segmentation with jobs-to-be-done** — students, young families and
   digital-native affluents are different customers
2. **Funnel-leak analysis** locating exactly where the group is lost — usually
   onboarding completion and salary-account activation
3. **Bundle design with one memorable hook** that is structurally hard for the
   rival to copy — for a co-operative: real ownership economics
4. **CAC-vs-CLV cohort arithmetic** with a payback year

**Storyline:** today's young customer is tomorrow's mortgage → three
sub-segments → the funnel leaks → the bundle and the hook → the CFO slide →
the legacy base protected → phased roadmap → risks and ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Judge attacks:** "Every bank says 'app plus youth account' — why is this
different?" → the hook and the segment sequencing · "You're abandoning your older
base" → the AND-not-OR slide · "Gen Z doesn't care about your co-op identity" →
concede the label, sell the substance · "CLV is speculative" → assumptions
on-slide, sensitivity in appendix.

**Traps:** the generic app answer · averaging the segments · a hook the incumbent
cannot deliver · spending without a payback horizon · forgetting the branch
network is both a cost problem and a trust asset.

**Self-check:** can a judge repeat your hook in one sentence afterwards?

# Type F · Talent, trust & change

## Recognise it

*Precedents: StandardAero 2024 (talent), People Corporation 2025 (trust), Manitoba Hydro 2023 (change).*

A large enterprise whose binding constraint is human — unfilled skilled roles,
internal distrust of a platform, change fatigue. The exhibits are workforce
demographics, adoption curves, engagement surveys.

**Core question:** which segmented people-programme moves the few groups that
actually matter — with measurable proof the organization can believe?

## What decides it

1. **Gap quantification and the cost of doing nothing** — lost contribution,
   overtime premium, attrition cost: computable and devastating
2. **Segmentation of the people** — graduates, mid-career skeptics,
   near-retirement experts, works council — and what moves each
3. A **programme portfolio with cost-per-net-outcome**, including one visibly
   rejected lever: the across-the-board raise that buys zero capacity
4. **Leading indicators before lagging ones**

**Storyline:** the priced gap → its decomposition → the four audiences → the
portfolio with €-per-outcome → the proof machine → governance path (works
council co-design) → roadmap with a visible 90-day win → risks and ask.

## Attacks, traps, self-check

**Judge attacks:** "This is an HR brochure" → cost per net productive FTE ·
"The union will block it" → co-design from day one, on the main deck ·
"You're fixing the tool, not the trust" → dedicated support, guarantees,
champions, relaunch · "How do we know it's working before year 2?" → leading
indicators with a monthly cadence.

**Traps:** generic employer branding · one message for all audiences · solving
the symptom instead of the trust · programmes with no owner · ignoring unionized
governance realities.

**Self-check:** does something visible happen for employees within 90 days?

# Using the templates

## At case release

1. **Duplicate** the matching template from `06-templates/case-type-templates/`
2. **Delete its red briefing slide**
3. **Rename** per the round's convention: "[Team Name] <Round> Case"
4. Replace every bracketed placeholder; keep the action-title discipline
5. **Re-check anonymity** — no school name, logo, or city hint anywhere
6. Recolour to the client's palette *only* if time remains after content freeze

## Two reminders

For the **boardroom** round, pair the type template with the summary-slide
layout in `06-templates/team_template.pptx` (slide 14) and the one-page brief in
`06-templates/judge_brief_template.docx`.

**Slide time follows the rubric.** Whatever the type, the implementation section
is load-bearing at 2.5× — **when in trouble, cut an analysis slide, never the
roadmap.**
