# Why we drill numbers

## Case math is a speed skill

- At Asper, **phones and smartwatches are banned** in the prep room
- **Practitioner judges trust teams that quantify on the spot**
- The 40 drills build speed on the calculations that appear in
  **almost every case**
- This deck teaches the **method** — the drills themselves live in the handout

## House rules — every answer, every time

- **Round aggressively**
- **State assumptions aloud**
- **Always give units**
- **Sanity-check against an anchor**
- Speak every answer **in English** — "one point two billion Canadian
  dollars," never "1,2 Milliarden"
- The false friend: German *Billion* = English **trillion** ·
  *Milliarde* = **billion**

# Anchors & toolkit

## Anchor numbers — memorize cold

| Anchor | Value | Anchor | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Germany population | 84M | Canada population | 40M |
| German households | 41M (~2.0 per HH) | Canadian households | 15M (~2.6 per HH) |
| Berlin | 3.7M | Manitoba | 1.4M |
| Munich | 1.5M | Winnipeg (metro) | 0.85M |
| EUR → CAD | ≈ 1.5 | Working days/year | 250 |
| German GDP | ≈ EUR 4.3T | Canadian GDP | ≈ CAD 2.9T |

## Fractions to percent, instantly

1/6 ≈ 17% · 1/7 ≈ 14% · 1/8 = 12.5% · 1/9 ≈ 11% · 1/12 ≈ 8%

Anchors are the sanity-check yardsticks — **anchor recall opens every
weekly session**, spoken in English, with units.

## Toolkit — speed shortcuts

- **Rule of 72:** years to double ≈ 72 ÷ growth rate ·
  **Rule of 114** for tripling
- **Flip trick:** a% of b = b% of a — 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4
- **Reciprocal shortcuts:** ÷0.8 = ×1.25 · ÷0.9 ≈ ×1.11 · ÷0.75 = ×1.33

## Toolkit — the classic traps

- **Successive changes multiply:** +30% then −30% = 1.3 × 0.7 = 0.91 →
  net −9%
- **Compounding approximation:** n years at rate g ≈ n·g + [n(n−1)/2]·g²
  total growth
- **Margin vs. markup:** margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) —
  a 25% markup is a 20% margin
- **pp vs. %:** 20% → 25% is +5 percentage points — a 25% relative increase

# Seven calculation families · A–G

## A · Market sizing (drills 1–8)

The shape: population → households → penetration → spend.

- 41M households × 25% with cats × 1.5 cats ≈ 15M cats;
  × EUR 300 → **≈ EUR 4.5B**
- **Cross-check bottom-up:** 15M cats × ~EUR 0.55 per day ≈ EUR 3B —
  quote the range, **name the driver**
- Judges buy **structure, not precision**
- Read the insight in the number — Manitoba candles ≈ CAD 11M:
  growth must come from outside the province

## B · Margins and pricing (drills 9–15)

- **Margin = profit ÷ price** · **markup = profit ÷ cost** —
  cost 40, price 50 → 20% margin, 25% markup. Never confuse the two.
- **Target price = cost ÷ (1 − margin):** cost 91, margin 35% →
  91 ÷ 0.65 = CAD 140
- **Blended margin** = revenue-weighted average
- **Price cuts bite:** −10% on price → +33% volume for the same profit
- **Operating leverage:** +10% revenue → +50% profit (5×)

## C · Growth and CAGR (drills 16–21)

- **Rule of 72:** 12% growth doubles revenue in 72 ÷ 12 = 6 years
- **Reverse CAGR by ratio:** 40 → 58 in 4 years is ratio 1.45;
  1.1⁴ ≈ 1.46 → **≈ 10% CAGR**
- Works downhill too: 120 → 90 in 3 years → ≈ −9% CAGR
- **Approximate compounding:** 7% for 5 years ≈ 35% simple +
  ~5% compounding → +40%

## D · Break-even (drills 22–27)

- **Units:** fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit —
  300k ÷ (25 − 15) = 30,000 units
- **Revenue:** fixed costs ÷ contribution margin —
  180k ÷ 0.30 = EUR 600k
- Same logic for a machine: CAD 240k ÷ CAD 8 saved = 30,000 units
- **Price moves it fast:** a 10% price cut raises break-even by 25%
- Product mix → weight the contributions by unit share

## E · Unit economics (drills 28–33)

- **Lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ monthly churn** — 4% churn → 25 months
- **LTV = ARPU × margin × lifetime** — 12 × 70% × 25 = EUR 210
- **LTV/CAC ≥ 3 is healthy** — 210 ÷ 70 = 3.0
- **CAC payback = CAC ÷ monthly contribution** — 42 ÷ 7 = 6 months
- Build the P&L per unit — one order, one store, one subscriber

## F · Quick NPV and payback (drills 34–37)

- **Perpetuity:** value = cash flow ÷ r — 150k ÷ 0.10 = 1.5M;
  minus 1.0M invested → **NPV +0.5M**
- **Growing perpetuity:** cash flow ÷ (r − g) —
  100k ÷ (0.12 − 0.02) = 1.0M
- **Memorize the 10% annuity factors:** 3 years ≈ 2.5 ·
  5 years ≈ 3.8 · 10 years ≈ 6.1
- **Payback:** cumulate cash flows until the investment is covered —
  e.g. ≈ 3.2 years

## G · Mental-math finishers (drills 38–40)

- **Flip it:** 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = **4**
- **Divide by multiplying:** 96 ÷ 0.8 = 96 × 1.25 = **120**
- **Sequential percentages never cancel:** +30% then −30% →
  1.3 × 0.7 = 0.91 → **net −9%**

These reflexes are what the timed gauntlet trains —
75 seconds per drill, no calculator.

# The weekly routine

## 30 minutes, every week

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Anchor recall** — quizzed in English, with units |
| 5–15 | **Two sizings aloud** — one German, one Canadian |
| 15–25 | **Timed gauntlet** — 8 drills from B–G, 75 s each |
| 25–30 | **Error log** — every miss re-derived, recorded |

- The partner **challenges one assumption** per sizing —
  defend it or adjust on the spot
- The error log becomes **next week's warm-up**

## Keeping the drills honest

- **Rotate the quizmaster** weekly
- **Once a month:** a full mock Q&A — the Chief Analyst defends a case
  model against **hostile numbers questions from the coach**
- Drills only count once they **survive contact with a judge**

## The standard for every answer

A **rounded number** · its **unit** · the **assumption** that produced
it · one **sanity check**.

> "Roughly CAD 11 million per year, assuming half of Manitoba's 560,000
> households spend 40 dollars — small enough that growth must come from
> outside the province."

**That sentence is the drill.**
