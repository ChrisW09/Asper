# Why drilled English

## What this workshop teaches

The competition is conducted **entirely in English** — every round, and
**every team member must speak in every round**.

- **Prepared case** — 25 minutes + 15 minutes Q&A
- **Boardroom** — 3-minute protected recommendation, then a **22-minute discussion**
- **Traditional** (2027: the 10-hour case) — 20 + 10 · **Final** — 20 + 15

This deck teaches **the system and the flagship phrases**. The full inventory
lives in the handout — drill from there.

## Two rules shape every sentence

- **Never identify HSBI to a judge** — instant disqualification. You present as
  your drawn team letter: "Team C".
- **Implementation & Resources weighs 2.5x** — your English must be sharpest
  where you talk **money, timelines, and resources**.

And the design principle behind the whole handout: under pressure you will only
retrieve **phrases of five to ten words**. Everything drilled is deliberately
short.

# 1 · Terminology

## The words that carry the score

Make these reflex first — the full DE–EN tables are in the handout.

| Deutsch | English |
| --- | --- |
| die Umsetzung | **implementation** — rubric weight 2.5x |
| der Umsatz | revenue — not "turnover" in N. America |
| die Handlungsempfehlung | recommendation |
| der Kapitalwert | net present value (NPV) |
| die Gewinnschwelle | break-even point |
| die Amortisationszeit | payback period — not "amortization" |

## False friends — the expensive ones

| German word | It means | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| die Milliarde | **billion** (10^9) | German "Billion" = **trillion** — off by 1,000 |
| die Provision | commission | English "provision" = Rückstellung |
| der Konzern | group, corporation | English "concern" = Sorge |
| eventuell | possibly, perhaps | "Eventually" is a promise, not an option |

Get "Milliarde" wrong and **your market size is off by a factor of 1,000**.

## False friends — the everyday ones

| German word | It means | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aktuell | current | "Actually" = tatsächlich — say "currently" |
| sensibel | sensitive | "Sensible" = vernünftig |
| konsequent | consistent, rigorous | "Consequently" = folglich |
| bekommen | to get, to receive | You *get* a discount; you do not become one |
| der Chef | boss, CEO | A "chef" cooks |

# 2 · Presentation phrases

## Openings — recommendation in minute one

Judges score "Framing the problem". Never name your school.

- "Our recommendation in one sentence: [X]. Here is why — and how."
- "We are Team C, and over the next twenty minutes we will show you how
  [Client] can [headline result]."
- "Three things today: the problem, our answer, and exactly how to make it
  happen."

## Signposting — one per section, one breath

- "Let me start with the problem."
- "That brings me to the financials."
- "So much for the market — now the money."
- "The key takeaway from this slide is simple: [X]."
- "And now the part that matters most: implementation."

## Handovers — everyone speaks

All four members must speak, so you will hand over at least three times.
**One sentence out, half a sentence in** — never re-introduce the deck.

- Out: "With that, I will hand over to Anna, who will walk you through the
  financials."
- Out: "For the numbers behind this, over to Jonas."
- In: "Thank you, Maria. As she said, the margin is the lever — here is how we
  pull it."
- In: "Thanks, Jonas. Building on that market picture, ..."

## Closing the ask

End on **implementation, cost, and payback** — the heaviest rubric line.
Yellow card = five minutes left; red card = one.

- "To sum up: we recommend [X]. It costs [Y], breaks even in [Z], and the
  first milestone is in April."
- "The ask today: approve [X] and a budget of [Y], and we start in Q2."
- "If you remember one number, make it this one: [n]."
- Under the red card: "I see the red card — let me close with the essentials:
  [three short points]."

# 3 · Q&A under fire

## Answer first, explain second

10–15 minutes in front of industry judges. Rule two: **short beats complete**.

- "Yes — and here is why."
- "No, and deliberately so: ..."
- "The short answer is [X]. The reasoning: ..."
- "Two things. First ... second ..."
- "We tested exactly that. The result was ..."

## Bridging & polite disagreement

Disagree with the premise, **never with the person**.

- "Fair point on the detail; the bigger picture is ..."
- "That is a fair challenge. We saw it differently, and here is why."
- "I would push back gently on one assumption there."
- Conceding well: "That is a good catch — our estimate is conservative there.
  The recommendation still holds because ..."

## Buying two seconds — without "ähm"

- "That is a fair question."
- Repeat the key noun back: "The rollout timeline. Two points."
- Or simply pause and breathe — **two seconds of silence read as composure;
  two seconds of "ähm" read as panic**.
- **Banned fillers** — they slip out in English too: "ähm", "genau", "also",
  "sozusagen", and the tag "…or?" — it does not exist in English.

## Missed the question? Ask.

- "I'm sorry, I didn't catch the last part — could you repeat it?"
- Paraphrase check: "If I understood correctly, you are asking about [X] — is
  that right?"
- Teammate rescue — agree on this beforehand: "I believe the question was
  about [X] — I can take that."

**Never bluff an answer you did not hear.** Asking once is professional;
guessing wrong is not.

# 4 · Boardroom register

## A conversation, not a lecture

After the protected three minutes: a **22-minute discussion** with the judges —
in past editions including a company representative. Every member must speak.
The register is **collegial and interactive**.

Inviting:

- "Happy to go deeper on the numbers if that is useful."
- "What would need to be true for this to be a yes for you?"
- "Is there a part of the plan you would like us to stress-test?"

## Build · redirect · bring in a teammate

- Building: "To build on that point ..." · "Exactly — and that is why we
  phased the rollout."
- Redirecting: "Can I park that for a moment and come back to it? I want to
  answer the funding question first."
- Bringing in a teammate — this is how everyone gets speaking time:
  "Lena owns this part of the model — Lena?" ·
  "Maria ran that analysis; she can give you the exact figure."

# 5 · Numbers out loud

## Six rules

Numbers are where German habits are most audible.

1. **Decimal point, not comma** — 3,5 becomes 3.5, "three point five"
2. **Percent never takes a plural** — a change is in **percentage points**
3. **Currency after the number** — "one point five million dollars"; first
   mention in Winnipeg: "Canadian dollars"
4. **Milliarde = billion · Billion = trillion** — drill until it is reflex
5. **Years** — 2027 is "twenty twenty-seven"
6. **Negatives & ranges** — "minus 340,000 dollars" · "five to seven percent"

## The daily dozen

Twelve practice sentences in the handout — **read aloud daily, record yourself
weekly**. Two samples:

- "Gross margin improved from 38.2% to 41.5% — an increase of 3.3 percentage
  points."
- "The Canadian market is worth 2.3 billion Canadian dollars [Milliarden!],
  growing at a CAGR of 6.1%."

# 6 · Pronunciation

## th · v · w

- **th** — tongue touches the teeth, never s, z, t, or d. Drill line:
  "We think this third growth path is worth three thousand."
- **v** = upper teeth on lower lip: *value, invest, revenue* ·
  **w** = rounded lips, no teeth: *we, would, win*
- Minimal pairs: *vest/west, vine/wine, verse/worse*. Drill line:
  "We will invest in value this winter."

## -ed endings & word stress

- **-ed** has three variants: /t/ (*launched*), /d/ — keep it voiced against
  German Auslautverhärtung (*planned*, not "plant"), /ɪd/ only after t or d
  (*implemented*). "Launched" has one syllable, not two.
- **Stress moves within word families**: **STRAT**-egy → stra-**TE**-gic ·
  a-**NAL**-y-sis → plural a-**NAL**-y-ses ("-seez") ·
  **COM**-pe-tent → com-pe-**TI**-tion ·
  de-**VEL**-op-ment — never "develop-MENT"

## Silent letters & the word ladder

- *debt* = "det" · *receipt* = "re-SEET" · *margin* = "MAR-jin" ·
  *niche* — "neesh" or "nitch", pick one and stay consistent ·
  *suite* = "sweet"
- **Daily two-minute drill** before every mock presentation: run the word
  ladder in the handout, then one full sentence: "Our strategic analysis shows
  a viable path to three percent growth, with the debt repaid by twenty
  twenty-nine."

# The routine

## Muscle memory, not vocabulary

- **One section per training week** — six sections, six weeks
- **English as the working language of every mock session**
- Numbers: the twelve sentences aloud daily, a recording weekly

By Winnipeg these phrases should be **muscle memory, not vocabulary**.
