# Why Q&A decides

## Scored in every round

| **Traditional case** | 10 minutes after the pitch |

Coaches across competitions describe Q&A as **the tiebreaker between closely
ranked teams**.

## Who is asking — and our standard

Asper's judges are **practitioners**: they probe **assumptions, feasibility,
and ownership**.

Our standard, every answer:

- inside **30 seconds**
- **answer first**
- **with a number**

# 1 · The answer-first protocol

## P-A-B-S — every answer

1. **Pause** (1–2 s) — signals thought, buys composure, lets the judge finish
2. **Answer** in the first sentence — "Yes, with one condition." / "2.1 years."
3. **Because** — one or two points, at least one number, ideally a slide reference
4. **Stop** — land the last word, hold the silence. No trailing off, no third reason.

Timing: **20–30 s** standard · **45 s maximum** for a financial walk-through
with an appendix slide on screen.

## When we don't know

**Never bluff.** The script:

> "We haven't calculated that precisely. Based on [case anchor], our estimate
> is [X]. It is the first thing we would validate in the pilot's 30-day review."

Honest gap **+ instant estimate + validation plan** scores. Improvised fiction
dies on the follow-up question.

## Hard rules

- **Never contradict a teammate** in front of judges — add nuance instead:
  "One addition —"
- **Never argue with a judge** — re-ground the discussion in the facts we
  presented
- **Fuzzy question?** Ask for clarification before answering it
- **Judge starts nodding?** Wrap up and invite the next question

# 2 · Bridging technique

## Back to prepared ground

**Acknowledge → Bridge → Anchor.**

| Move | Phrase bank |
| --- | --- |
| **Acknowledge** | "Fair challenge." · "Exactly the right question." |
| **Bridge** | "And that is why…" · "The case data tells us…" |
| **Anchor** | Sensitivity table · phasing gate · appendix · thesis |

## Worked example — and the limit

*"Isn't this too expensive for a mid-sized client?"*

> "Fair — cash is the constraint. That is why the plan self-funds after month
> nine: Phase 1 needs only CAD 400k, Phase 2 is paid from Phase 1 margin.
> Appendix 6 shows the cash curve."

**Bridging steers — it never evades.** The Answer step always comes first; a
dodge that skips it is instantly visible to practitioner judges.

# 3 · The quarterback system

## One member runs traffic

The **Quarterback** — our PM. Nobody else decides who answers.

- **Domain questions** → their owner, by name: "Our numbers are Lena's — Lena?"
- **Ambiguous or hostile** → the Quarterback takes first contact, answers or
  reframes, then routes
- **One voice per answer** — a second member may add exactly one sentence, only
  if it adds new value. Pile-ons are banned.

## Ownership map

| Role | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| **Quarterback** (PM) | Scope, prioritization, implementation, politics |
| **Chief Analyst** | All financials and assumptions |
| **Strategist** | Market, competition, "why not X?" |
| **Design & Data Lead** | Customer, data, tech, operations detail |

## Airtime and rescue

- **Coverage tracking** — in boardroom formats a silent member can sink the
  team; steer questions to anyone running light: "Max built that — Max?"
- **Rescue protocol** — a teammate stalls: no correction, no rescue-grab. The
  Quarterback bridges — "Let me add the context behind Lena's point —" — gives
  two seconds to recover, then hands back if possible

## The DJ and the close

- **The DJ** — the Design & Data Lead pulls numbered appendix slides on demand;
  every financial answer lands with its exhibit on screen **within five seconds**
- **The close** — if the moderator allows, the Quarterback ends Q&A with a
  **20-second recap** of recommendation and impact. The last words the judges
  hear are ours.

# 4 · The question taxonomy

## Five banks · 60 hard questions

| Bank | Attacks |
| --- | --- |
| **A · Financial challenge** | Growth, payback, CAC, funding, FX |
| **B · Feasibility attack** | Timeline, headcount, IT, partners, scale |
| **C · Ethics & risk** | Pricing fairness, data, layoffs, abort triggers |
| **D · Curveball** | Zero budget, Plan B, "weakest part?" |
| **E · Hostile** | "Your numbers are wrong." — composure tests |

12 each, in the handout. Model answers follow the first three of each group;
drill the rest against the same patterns.

## A · Financial challenge

*"Your projection assumes 8% growth; the case says 3%. Reconcile that."*

> "The market grows 3%; our 8% is that plus five points of share gain from the
> two channels on slide 9. If we win zero share, NPV falls from 4.1M to 1.9M —
> still positive."

**Patterns:** decompose the number, show the floor still clears · number
instantly, then the built-in control · own the assumption, derive it from case
data, show the sensitivity.

## B · Feasibility attack

*"Your timeline is 12 months; this client has never executed in under 24."*

> "We planned against their track record, not the best case. The 12 months
> covers only a one-region pilot using existing staff; national rollout is
> months 12–24. The critical path is IT — it starts in week one."

**Patterns:** shrink the claim to what fits the window · named roles, counted
heads, costed · an architecture answer plus a costed fallback.

## C · Ethics and risk

*"You are pushing premium prices onto the customers who can least afford them."*

> "Affordability was a design constraint: the entry product stays at today's
> price — premiumization targets the gifting segment, 70% of value. Net of that
> guardrail, margin still gains three points."

**Patterns:** show the safeguard was designed in, quantify net of it · name the
regime, show it costed and scheduled · humane, specific, provisioned.

## D · Curveball

*"Sell me your recommendation in one sentence."*

> "[Client] should [action], because it converts [existing asset] into
> [quantified outcome] within [timeframe], with downside capped by [gate]."

**Pre-write this sentence for every case** — it also opens the boardroom pitch.

Other patterns: separate process moves from capital moves · same destination,
adjusted governance.

## E · Hostile

*"Your numbers are simply wrong."*

Pause, stay warm, invite specificity: "Happy to walk through them — which
figure shall we start with?" Then re-derive that one figure from case data with
the appendix slide up.

**Patterns:** never defend in general — force one number, win that number ·
agree with the history, differentiate on mechanism · humility, invite the
expertise, protect the core.

## House rules under fire

- Voice **low and slow**
- The smile stays **professional, never smug**
- Hostility is **a composure test**, not an information request — judges watch
  how we treat each other and them
- We never argue, never sulk, never fold: **acknowledge, answer, anchor**

# 5 · How the drills run

## Five formats

| Format | Mechanics |
| --- | --- |
| **Rapid fire** (10 min) | Coach fires from the banks, 30-s intervals |
| **Red team** | Alumni/coach as hostile board — best calibration |
| **Distraction drill** | Answer through rustling, mid-sentence beeps |
| **10-hardball set** | Pre-written before every presentation |
| **Film review** | Record every mock Q&A; check discipline, pace |

Rapid fire rule: any answer over 30 seconds or without a number is **repeated**.

## The 10-hardball set — pre-written every time

- Biggest assumption risk
- The recommendation at half the projected market growth
- "Why not just cut costs?"
- Competitor response
- Feasibility
- Market-size validation
- Timeline and resources
- Stakeholder buy-in
- Comparison to rejected alternatives
- Funding source

## Now we drill

- The **60 questions** are in the handout — 12 per bank, A through E
- Model answers cover the first three of each group; the rest are drilled
  against the same patterns
- The standard stands for every answer: **30 seconds · answer first · with a
  number**
