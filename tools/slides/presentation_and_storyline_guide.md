# What we are optimising for

## The rubric decides the deck

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| **Implementation & resources** | **2.5×** |
| Analysis · Recommendation | 2.0× each |
| Communication · Framing · Impact · Risks | 1.0× each |

Asper's judges are practitioners, not academics. Everything that follows is
tuned to one outcome: **one decisive, quantified, executable recommendation
with a resourced roadmap.**

## Three rounds, three shapes

| Round | Prep | Delivery | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepared | ~2 weeks | 25 min + 15 min Q&A | 25 % |
| Boardroom | 4 h on site | 3-min pitch + 22-min discussion | 30 % |
| Traditional | 3 h on site → **10 h in 2027** | 20 min + 10 min Q&A | 45 % |

# 1 · Action titles

## Vertical and horizontal logic

- Every slide title is a **full-sentence claim**, with a number wherever
  possible — never a label
- The slide body exists **only to prove its title**. That is *vertical* logic
- Read top to bottom, **the titles alone must tell the whole argument**. That is
  *horizontal* logic
- Judges skim. Many form their verdict from titles and charts alone

## Every title is a claim, with a number

| Weak (label) | Strong (action title) |
| --- | --- |
| Market Overview | The premium candle market grows 6 % p.a. — but 70 % of value sits in gifting |
| Financial Projections | A CAD 1.2M investment returns CAD 3.4M NPV and pays back in 2.1 years |
| Risks | Channel conflict and FX are capped at CAD 0.4M by our phasing |

## The 60-second test

Before any rehearsal, one member reads **only the titles** aloud.

If a listener cannot reproduce the recommendation, its three reasons, the cost
and the timeline from titles alone — **rewrite the titles before touching
anything else.**

## Slide discipline

- One idea per slide; chart-led, not text-led
- A judge grasps each visual in **under five seconds**
- Two to three colours, consistent throughout
- Quantify every claim: "raises EBITDA margin 3.2 pp, adding CAD 4.7M by 2029"
  beats "improves profitability"
- Rough estimates with disclosed assumptions beat vague adjectives

# 2 · The storyline skeleton

## One skeleton, every deck — slides 1 to 6

| Slide | Job |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Title | Client, date; recommendation teased in the subtitle |
| 2 Executive summary | Recommendation, three reasons, quantified impact, the ask |
| 3 Situation & problem | The one-sentence exam question, and why now |
| 4–6 Analysis | One insight per slide, proving reasons 1–3 |

## And slides 7 to the appendix

| Slide | Job |
| --- | --- |
| 7 Recommendation detail | What exactly, for whom, at what price and scope |
| 8 Financials | Investment, returns, NPV/payback, sensitivity band |
| 9–10 Implementation | 30/60/90 days plus a phased roadmap, owners, resources |
| 11 Risks | Top three, each with mitigation and trigger point |
| 12 Close | Recommendation and impact in one line |
| A1–A15+ | Appendix: numbered Q&A ammunition |

## Two rules about that table

**Implementation gets two slides** because practitioner judges score execution
hardest — it is the 2.5× category.

**The appendix is a weapon, not a dump.** Every appendix slide is built to
answer a predicted question, and numbered so we can call it up in seconds.

One recommendation, never a menu: *"We recommend B, and here is why A and C
don't hold up."*

# 3 · The 3-hour build

## Roles

| Role | Profile | Owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quarterback | General business admin | Time, storyline, exec summary, implementation, final calls |
| Chief Analyst | Finance | The model, every case number, financial + sensitivity slides |
| Strategist | Innovation | Alternatives, recommendation design, risks, the creative edge |
| Design & Data | Computer science | Template, charts, assembly, appendix index, slide DJ |

Time split: ~15 % reading · 25 % structured debate · 50 % parallel build ·
10–15 % rehearsal.

## The two gates

| Clock | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:30 | Prompt read aloud twice; silent read by role |
| 0:30–0:55 | Readouts, then debate → pick ONE alternative |
| **0:55** | **GATE 1 — recommendation locked. Never reopened.** |
| 0:55–1:05 | The 12 action titles written and approved |
| 1:05–2:00 | Parallel build; numbers locked by 1:30 |
| **2:00** | **GATE 2 — every draft slide is in the deck.** |
| 2:20–2:35 | Title read-through; numbers verified; consistency scrub |
| 2:35–2:55 | Rehearsal 1 rough, Rehearsal 2 timed; three hardballs each |
| 2:55–3:00 | Freeze; export; pre-room ritual |

## What makes it work

- The Quarterback **vocalizes time and drift**: "Twenty minutes gone — we are
  still on issues"
- Consensus always, but the Quarterback breaks ties instantly
- Anyone who finishes early builds appendix ammunition
- **Rehearsal time is sacred.** An average deck delivered brilliantly beats a
  brilliant deck delivered raw
- Boardroom (4 h): stretch the build block, add a Q&A-only rehearsal
- Prepared: same skeleton, then iterate with the coach across two weeks

## 2027: the 10-hour case

Same 20 + 10 delivery, same 45 % weight (assumed until confirmed) — **the
3-hour workflow stays the core**. What judges will expect from ten hours:

- **Primary-style research** — sourced facts, not assumptions
- **Fuller financials** — three scenarios, sensitivity, payback/NPV, funding
- **A sharper implementation plan** — owners, months, budget by phase, KPIs,
  a stop/go gate
- **An appendix bank** — 15–25 numbered slides, one per predicted question

More proof and more rehearsal — **not more main slides.**

## The 10-hour clock

| Clock | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–1:30 | Read and frame; provisional call + written research plan |
| 1:30–3:30 | Research and first analysis, 30 min per thread |
| **3:30** | **GATE A — direction confirmed**; lunch away from screens |
| 4:20–6:00 | Deep analysis: model, sensitivities, implementation, KPIs |
| **6:00** | **GATE B — storyline frozen**; ghost deck in template |
| 6:00–8:30 | Build + appendix bank; number audit; **freeze at 8:30** |
| 8:30–10:00 | Timed rehearsal, Q&A routine, lockdown, buffer |

Authoritative clock: `03-team/role_playbooks.md`, "The 10-hour day".
First run: W17 (Sparkasse) · again in the W25 competition-week simulation.

# 4 · The boardroom pitch

## Three minutes, ~400 words, memorised

| Clock | Beat | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:20 | Governing thesis | "We recommend [action]. It delivers [impact] by [date] for [X]." |
| 0:20–0:50 | Problem, sharpened | The insight that reframes the case — why now, in one number |
| 0:50–2:10 | Three pillars | Reasons 1–3, ~25 s each, one number each, one speaker each |
| 2:10–2:40 | Money and motion | Investment, return, payback, first 90 days |
| 2:40–3:00 | Risk + invitation | "The main risk is X; we cap it by Y. We'd like to pressure-test A, B, C with you." |

One summary slide only — appendices are blocked during the protected three
minutes. This is the one script we memorize verbatim and never improvise.

## The closing invitation is not decoration

Board-presentation research is unambiguous: **directors engage when you ask for
their input on named decision points**, and disengage when you fish for
applause.

Those three named areas also plant the agenda for the 22 minutes that follow —
the discussion usually starts on ground we chose.

# 5 · The 22-minute discussion

## Behave like colleagues, not defendants

- **Everyone speaks, by design.** Teams rank last if a member stays silent.
  Pre-assign domains: Quarterback frames and closes · Analyst owns every number ·
  Strategist owns market and competitive logic · Design & Data owns customer,
  data and technology
- **Appendix as ammunition.** The slide DJ has the right exhibit on screen
  within five seconds: "Appendix 7 shows exactly that sensitivity"
- **Build, don't defend.** Open with "Yes, and —" or "Fair — and here is how we
  handled it." Never "No"

## Two more habits

- **Re-anchor.** Every three or four exchanges, someone ties the thread back to
  the governing thesis in one line. Offered a final word, the Quarterback closes
  in 20 seconds: recommendation, impact, first step.
- **Posture.** Forearms on the table, lean slightly in, notepad out, no laptops
  open except the DJ's. Note judges' points visibly and reference them later —
  *"Coming back to your margin concern…"*. When a judge starts nodding, finish
  the sentence and stop.

# 6 · Handovers

## The baton formula

Sloppy transitions are a documented failure mode at every major competition —
and the cheapest one to fix.

> "…so the market is ready. How much value that creates — Lena has the numbers."
>
> "Thanks, Jonas. Three numbers matter: 1.2 million invested, 3.4 million
> returned, 2.1 years to payback."

## The rules

- Never end a section on a filler sentence
- The clicker travels **during** the name, not after it
- The incoming speaker steps forward as the name is spoken — motion signals
  structure
- Equal airtime for all four members, in every round
- Rehearse handovers as their own drill: **five consecutive clean passes**
  before any full run-through counts

# 7 · Delivering in English

## Our English is an asset, managed deliberately

- **Slow is credible.** 130–140 words per minute. Pause instead of filling —
  silence reads as confidence, "ähm" does not
- **Short sentences.** Cap spoken sentences at roughly 15 words; English rewards
  subject–verb–object
- **Memorize the load-bearing lines verbatim:** the first 30 seconds, every
  handover, the closing line, the one-sentence recommendation. Improvise the
  middle, never the frame
- **Never apologize for your English.** Structure and precision outrank
  pronunciation

## Kill the Germanisms

| Instead of | Say |
| --- | --- |
| "We would like to present" | "We will show" |
| "Until Friday" | "By Friday" |
| "Actual" (meaning current) | "Current" |
| "Chance" | "Opportunity" |
| German *Billion* | **trillion** — *Milliarde* is billion |

Numbers aloud, daily: decimal **point**, not comma — "two point one years".
Banned fillers: *basically, actually, you know*.

# 8 · Presence

## Dress and stance

- **Attire.** Business formal, coordinated as a team, harmonized with the deck's
  colours. Two complete outfits plus a stain-and-repair kit — competition weeks
  are long
- **Standing.** Weight balanced, no pacing, no lectern-gripping; gestures at
  chest height; eye contact swept across *all* judges, not only the friendly one
- **Seated.** Upright, hands visible, forearms on the table

## Energy and logistics

- Practitioner judges read presence in the **first 30 seconds**. Smile at the
  open and the close
- Manage fuel deliberately — nothing experimental on competition days — and
  protect sleep: delivery visibly collapses after short-sleep nights
- Visit the presentation room before round one; know where the restrooms are so
  no prep minute is lost
- **The pre-room ritual:** 60 seconds, top three keys spoken aloud, one breath
  together, walk in
- **After the buzzer:** leave the outcome in the room. Reset, refuel, go again
