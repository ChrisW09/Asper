## The competition in 60 seconds

Graduate teams of four (nine in 2025 and 2026) compete over a six-day week in **three case formats**, then a
final round. Everything runs **in English**, PowerPoint only, judged by ~50 industry practitioners.

| Day (2027) | Round | Prep | Delivery | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tue Feb 16 | **Prepared case** | released ~2 weeks ahead, all resources incl. coach | 25 min presentation + 15 min Q&A; deck due as `.pptx` by e-mail before arrival (**late = bottom of pool**) | **25 %** |
| Wed Feb 17 | **Boardroom case** | 4 h on site, no coach contact | 3-min *protected* pitch from **one summary slide** + 22-min judge discussion; one-page b/w judge brief handed to judges in advance | **30 %** |
| Fri Feb 19 → Sat Feb 20 | **10-hour case** *(the traditional round; 3 h until 2026)* | 10 h on site, no coach contact | 20 min presentation + 10 min Q&A; unlimited slides | **45 %** |
| Sat Feb 20 | **Final** | top 3 by weighted score; **decks frozen** | 20 + 15 to a fresh panel | decides the winner |

Mon Feb 15 is arrival (registration and division draw expected), Thu Feb 18 an excursion day, and the awards
dinner closes Saturday. The 10-hour row is our reading of the published schedule — the rules text on the same
page still says "traditional case" without a prep length — and the organizers have been asked to confirm.

Standing conditions: internet **and Generative AI allowed** during prep · one laptop per student, no
phones or smartwatches · prebuilt own templates permitted · every team member must speak in every round ·
yellow card at 5 min, red at 1 min · **naming your school to a judge = disqualification**.

Field and history: 2025 saw Asper, Beedie, DeGroote, HEC Montréal, Ivey, John Molson, Levene, Memorial,
Telfer. 2026 (fourth edition) included the first international team — Babeș-Bolyai, Romania — which **won
outright**, ahead of DeGroote and HEC Montréal. That is the precedent this team is following.

Full validated rule base: [`01-competition/facts_and_rules.md`](01-competition/facts_and_rules.md) —
**the single source of truth** for every rule claim in every other document.

## The rubric — and what it means for training

The official ABCC rubric (2025 Event Guide) applies to **all rounds**, 7 categories, max 105 points:

| Category | Weight | What judges ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Implementation & resources** | **2.5×** | Is there a clear path to follow, with resources, time, and financial considerations? |
| Analysis | 2.0× | Is the assessment substantiated by research, logic, quantitative and qualitative analysis? |
| Recommendation | 2.0× | Effective, clear, comprehensive, strategically fitting? |
| Communicating effectively | 1.0× | Clear, professional, compelling delivery and a readable deck? |
| Framing the problem | 1.0× | Are issues wrapped into a clear, solvable summary (primary + secondary)? |
| Impact | 1.0× | Quantitative and qualitative benefit to the organization? |
| Measurement, risks & mitigation | 1.0× | Progress measurement and realistic risk mitigation? |

**Training implication.** Implementation & Resources alone is worth more than any other category — 2.5× is
nearly a quarter of the sheet. Every mock presentation must end with a **concrete, resourced, costed
implementation plan with owners, timing, and KPIs**. A 10-hour case raises the bar on exactly this category
and on Analysis: judges will expect real research and fuller financials, not a 3-hour sketch. Drill it in
[`04-training/exercises/workbook3_storyline_and_slides.md`](04-training/exercises/workbook3_storyline_and_slides.md).

## Rules cheat sheet: AI, coach, anonymity

**AI is explicitly allowed** (2025 Event Guide; the 2027 page is silent on it). The rules permit internet use
during timed prep, including "any login-required website … filesharing websites, **and Generative AI**." Over
a 10-hour day that matters more, not less.

Because every team has the same arsenal, the edge is a **drilled procedure**, not access
([`03-team/role_playbooks.md`](03-team/role_playbooks.md), GenAI SOP): one designated operator (CS &
analytics lead), narrow sanctioned tasks, and the hard rule that **no unverified number reaches a slide**.
Framing, option choice, and the recommendation are never outsourced — borrowed logic collapses in the
22-minute discussion. **Confidentiality caution:** competition cases are confidential documents, so do not
paste full case text into consumer AI tools; prefer university-licensed enterprise tools and work from your
own summaries.

**The coach is in the game** for the prepared case (unrestricted — faculty input explicitly allowed), for
everything before the competition, and on site for presentations, feedback sessions, the excursion, and the
full social program.

**The coach goes silent** from the moment a **boardroom** or **10-hour** case is released until the team has
presented — for the 10-hour case that is the whole Friday prep day through Saturday's presentation. Any
communication between coach and team during a presentation or Q&A, or any coach intervention in the question
period, means **immediate disqualification**. Advisors of finalist teams may not communicate with their team
until after it has presented.

**Anonymity binds everyone, coach included.** Nothing said, worn, or shown may identify HSBI. The team
competes under **a fictional consulting name it chooses itself** and registers with the organizers, plus a
**division letter A–I drawn at on-site registration** that sets the week's slots. Identifying your school to a
judge = disqualification. **Never commit the HSBI name into a slide template or judge brief.**
