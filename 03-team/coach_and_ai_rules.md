# Two Questions, Answered from the Official Rules

Quick-reference for the coach and the team. Source: official ABCC 2025 Event Guide (the fullest published rulebook) and the 2027 website; re-verify both points the day the 2027 Competition Guide arrives. Full rule base: `01-competition/facts_and_rules.md`. **2027 update (2026-10-31):** the published schedule runs Mon Feb 15 – Sat Feb 20 and lists the third round as a **10-hour case** (prep Fri Feb 19, presentations Sat Feb 20) — assumed to replace the 3-hour traditional case until the organizers confirm.

## 1. May the team use AI during the competition?

**Yes — explicitly.** The official rules allow internet use during timed prep, including "any login-required website, including password-protected databases from their University, filesharing websites, **and Generative AI**." That covers the 4-hour boardroom prep and the third-round prep — 3 hours in 2025/2026, a **10-hour case** in 2027 (assume the clause carries over; it is on our open-questions list); in the prepared case (worked on at home), all resources are allowed anyway.

Practical consequences, from our GenAI standard operating procedure (`03-team/role_playbooks.md`):

- Every team has the same arsenal, so the edge is a **drilled procedure**, not access: one designated operator (CS & analytics lead), narrow sanctioned tasks (checking math, stress-testing the recommendation, generating counterarguments, polishing brief wording, the 10-minute Q&A-anticipation routine), and the hard rule that **no unverified number reaches a slide**.
- The core thinking — framing, option choice, the recommendation — is never outsourced; judges interrogate the team, and borrowed logic collapses in the 22-minute discussion.
- **Confidentiality caution:** the cases themselves are confidential competition documents ("no reproduction or transmission"), so avoid pasting full case text into consumer AI tools; prefer university-licensed enterprise tools, work from your own summaries, and check the 2027 guide for any new AI clauses before travelling.
- Devices still bound: one laptop per student, no phones or smartwatches; volunteers check prep rooms.

## 2. What may the professor/coach do — and not do?

**Where the coach is fully in the game:**

- **Prepared case (release → submission):** unrestricted involvement — "teams may use all resources at their disposal," explicitly including faculty and coach input. Reviewing storylines, challenging the model, running rehearsals: all allowed and expected. (Only sponsor-employee contact is banned, for everyone.)
- **Before the competition:** everything in this repository — training, simulations, feedback, selection, logistics — plus registering the team and handling all organizer communication.
- **On site:** attending presentations (all teams', once the room is entered the coach stays for that room's full slate and steps out only during judge deliberations), the judges' feedback sessions alongside the team, and the entire social program — the networking is part of the coach's job.

**Where the coach must go silent:**

- From the moment the **boardroom** or **third-round** case is released until the team has presented: **no contact with the team whatsoever** — not in person, not by message. The team is on its own for the full prep window and presentation. In 2027 that means **the whole 10-hour prep day (Fri Feb 19) and the Saturday morning until the team has presented** — including meals, corridors and the evening in between. Plan your own Friday accordingly; the team's habits, not your advice, carry them through it.
- **Excursion day (Thu Feb 18)** is a social day, not a round: contact is allowed (the boardroom is over, the 10-hour case not yet released), but anonymity still binds everyone — judges and volunteers may be on the same excursion.
- During any presentation or Q&A: **any communication between coach and team — or any coach intervention in the question period — means immediate disqualification of the team.**
- If the team reaches the **final**: advisors of finalist teams may not communicate with their team until after it has presented (finalists wait in a holding room; the third-round deck is frozen — in 2027 the finals run Sat Feb 20, the same day as the 10-hour presentations).

**One more coach-relevant rule:** the school-anonymity rule binds the coach too — nothing said to a judge, worn, or shown may identify HSBI. Teams compete under **a fictional consulting name they choose themselves** and register with the organizers before the event, plus **a division letter (A–I) drawn at on-site registration** that sets the week's presentation slots. The name is ours; the letter is theirs.

**Rule of thumb for February:** the coach's influence ends at case release and resumes at the feedback session — everything the team needs from you must already be in their habits by then. That is what the autumn simulation calendar (`02-plan/week_by_week_plan.md`) is for.
