# ABCC 2027 Role Playbooks — Team HSBI

**Audience:** the four HSBI competitors and the coach. **Scope:** who does what, minute by minute, in the timed rounds, plus our generative-AI operating procedure and the prep-room kit. Facts follow the 2025 Event Guide baseline in `01-competition/facts_and_rules.md`; re-verify against the 2027 rules package when published.

**Context in one paragraph.** The fifth annual ABCC runs 18–22 February 2027 at the Drake Centre, Asper School of Business, Winnipeg — nine teams of four. Three scored rounds: the **prepared case** (25 %; 25-minute presentation + 15-minute Q&A, submitted as .pptx by e-mail; late submissions go to the bottom of the pool), the **boardroom case** (30 %; 4-hour on-site prep, then a **protected 3-minute recommendation on one summary slide** with appendices blocked, followed by a **22-minute discussion** with appendices accessible; judges receive our **one-page black-and-white executive brief** in advance), and the **traditional case** (45 %; 3-hour on-site prep, 20-minute presentation + 10-minute Q&A). The top three reach a final with **frozen decks** (20 + 15); ties break on the higher traditional-case score — **traditional day is where the competition is won** (2025 pattern put it on Saturday; the Thu–Mon 2027 footprint most likely puts it on Sunday — confirm with the 2027 guide). Every member must speak in every round (in the boardroom, during the discussion). During on-site prep: internet, university databases, file sharing and **generative AI are allowed**; one laptop per student; no phones or smartwatches; no coach or outside contact after release; identifying our school to a judge means disqualification. Timing cards: yellow at 5 minutes, red at 1.

## The shared team clock

All four playbooks run on the same six phases so hand-offs are predictable. Slots are staggered and **late arrival earns no extra time**; the clock starts the second the case is handed over.

| Phase | Traditional (3 h) | Boardroom (4 h) | Team output at gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Read & frame | 0:00–0:20 | 0:00–0:25 | Everyone has read; key facts logged |
| P2 Align & decide | 0:20–0:40 | 0:25–0:55 | One-sentence recommendation locked |
| P3 Analysis sprint | 0:40–1:40 | 0:55–2:15 | Model, evidence, concept detail done |
| P4 Build | 1:40–2:25 | 2:15–3:15 | Deck (+ brief) assembled in template |
| P5 Verify & rehearse | 2:25–2:50 | 3:15–3:45 | Numbers audited; Q&A grid filled; run-through |
| P6 Lockdown & USB | 2:50–3:00 | 3:45–4:00 | Files named, saved to provided USB, verified |

Two hard gates: the direction locked at P2 is reopened only by the strategist (at an announced 10-minute cost), and a **number freeze** starts at P6 — after it, nothing new goes on a slide.

## 1. Finance lead

**Mission.** Own every number the team shows or says. The heaviest rubric line, *Implementation & resources* (2.5×), explicitly asks for financial and resource considerations, and *Analysis* (2.0×) demands quantitative substantiation — so this role converts every idea into costs, benefits, payback and a funded plan. Nothing financial reaches a slide unless this role produced or verified it.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| Prepared (25 %) | Full financial model workbook; impact exhibit (payback/NPV); costed implementation budget; sensitivity table |
| Boardroom (30 %) | The three headline figures on the single summary slide; financial paragraph of the one-page brief; 3–5 financial appendix slides; costed resourcing plan |
| Traditional (45 %) | Quantified-impact slide; implementation budget and resourcing slide; financial appendices; assumptions register |
| Final | Nothing new (decks frozen) — total command of every number for the 15-minute Q&A |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | Traditional (3 h) | Boardroom (4 h) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Read financial exhibits first; log every given figure into the assumptions register | Same, with 5 extra minutes on the client's baseline economics |
| P2 | Two-minute "size of the prize" estimate per option to inform the vote | Same; flag which option we can credibly cost in 4 hours |
| P3 | Build the model from the prebuilt shell: baseline, option economics, implementation costs; push interim outputs to the strategist | Same, deeper: add sensitivity and a funding/resourcing view for the brief |
| P4 | Turn the model into 2–3 slides (impact, budget, sensitivity); supply exec-summary numbers | Deliver max three headline figures for the summary slide; write the brief's financial lines; build the financial appendix |
| P5 | **Number audit:** recompute every displayed figure from source; run the AI math-check lane; brief the team on the three numbers judges will attack | Same, plus rehearse a "walk me through your model" appendix path for the discussion |
| P6 | Freeze the assumptions register; tie out final deck against the workbook | Same, plus confirm brief tables read in black-and-white print |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) Model-shell speed drill: blank prebuilt shell to working option economics in 45 minutes. (2) Mental-math and clean-rounding drill for live Q&A. (3) Rebuild the numbers for a past case (e.g., Access Credit Union 2026) end-to-end. (4) Sensitivity one-pager in 10 minutes. (5) Coach-led assumption gauntlet: defend every input for 15 minutes.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Over-modeling.** The model becomes an end in itself. → Hard gate: slide-ready outputs by end of P3; the strategist may call "good enough" and it stands.
- **Orphan numbers.** Slide edits drift from the model. → One workbook is the single source of truth; number freeze at P6 with a final tie-out.
- **False precision.** "€1,247,332" invites attack. → Round aggressively, show ranges, label assumptions.
- **Freezing on an assumption challenge.** → The assumptions register carries a rationale and a sensitivity for each input: "at half our uptake, payback moves from 18 to 30 months — the recommendation holds."

## 2. Innovation lead

**Mission.** Own the distinctiveness and concreteness of the recommendation. *Recommendation* weighs 2.0× and nine teams see the same case; recent clients explicitly wanted boldness (Jazz Winnipeg: a "radical new operational idea"; Coal & Canary: premium to luxury; Access Credit Union: youth-engaging service innovations). Generate wide, kill fast, make the survivor vivid and implementable.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| Prepared (25 %) | Option tree with kill log; concept slides (what it is, why this client, why now); differentiation story; pilot design |
| Boardroom (30 %) | The recommendation's core wording and label on the summary slide; counterargument prep sheet for the discussion; concept appendix |
| Traditional (45 %) | Concept and "why this wins" slide; alternatives-considered slide; pilot/rollout design input |
| Final | Sharpened articulation; rehearsed answers to every "why not X?" |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | Traditional (3 h) | Boardroom (4 h) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Read for customer, market and constraint signals; draft 5–7 option stubs | Same, plus note what the client tried before (recent boardroom panels included a company representative) |
| P2 | Pitch each stub in 30 seconds; run the kill; lock direction with the strategist | Same, plus draft the one-sentence recommendation for the protected 3 minutes |
| P3 | Make the idea concrete: features, customer journey, differentiation; pull precedent with the CS lead; feed sizing inputs to finance | Same, deeper: define the pilot and the "day one" experience |
| P4 | Build concept and alternatives-considered slides (considered alternatives score under *Analysis*) | Own the recommendation wording on the summary slide; build the concept appendix |
| P5 | Red-team own idea via the AI counterargument lane; script "why not the obvious alternative?" answers | Same, tuned for the interactive 22-minute discussion |
| P6 | Final coherence read: does every slide serve the idea's story? | Same |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) Ten ideas in ten minutes, weekly. (2) Kill-criteria drill: impact/feasibility/fit screen in 5 minutes. (3) Analogy transfer: adapt a proven model from an adjacent industry in 15 minutes. (4) "So what?" chains: any feature to client benefit in three steps. (5) Regenerate options for past ABCC cases; compare against what strong teams chose.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Falling in love with idea #1.** → Minimum five options and a written kill log before P2 lock — no exceptions.
- **Clever but unimplementable.** The heaviest rubric line is implementation (2.5×). → Every concept element must map to a resourced step in the strategist's plan; unmappable elements are cut or parked as "phase 2".
- **Reopening the decision mid-build.** → Only the strategist can reopen, at an announced 10-minute cost.
- **A generic answer eight other teams will give.** → P3 differentiation check: "Which two teams will say exactly this — and what is our twist?"

## 3. CS & analytics lead

**Mission.** Own the evidence and the machinery: fastest researcher, data wrangler, chart builder, deck mechanic, and designated AI operator. *Analysis* (2.0×) rewards research plus quantitative and qualitative rigor; this role also owns file compliance (.pptx only, exact file names such as "[Team Name] Traditional Case", USB handover at the fixed pickup time) so nobody else thinks about mechanics.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| Prepared (25 %) | Research pack with source log; data exhibits and charts; master deck file; the on-time submission e-mail ("[Team Name] Prepared Case") |
| Boardroom (30 %) | Numbered appendix map so any judge question reaches its appendix in seconds; charts for slide and brief; USB save |
| Traditional (45 %) | Benchmark table with sources; exhibits; master deck; USB save under the exact required file name |
| Final | Printed Q&A grid and appendix map (deck edits are impossible) |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | Traditional (3 h) | Boardroom (4 h) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Stand up the workspace: shared files, prebuilt neutral template open, visible timer; extract case exhibit data into a working sheet | Same |
| P2 | Rapid market/benchmark scan to inform the vote; open the source log | Same |
| P3 | On-demand research sprints (customer stats, comparables) — everything into the source log; build charts for finance and innovation; operate the team AI lane per the SOP below | Same, plus one deeper data pull the 3-hour round cannot afford |
| P4 | Rolling deck merge: pull everyone's slides into the template, fix formatting, number the appendices | Same, plus lay out the one-page brief in black-and-white and print-test contrast |
| P5 | Run the compliance checklist (file name and type; zero school identifiers in slides, template or metadata; slide numbers; appendix map); execute the 10-minute AI Q&A routine with the strategist | Same, plus rehearse driving appendices with the provided wireless keyboard and mouse so discussion navigation is instant |
| P6 | Save to the provided USB, open it from the stick to verify, keep a backup copy | Same, for both deck and brief |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) 15-minute research sprint with a complete source log. (2) Chart-in-five-minutes: case table to clean exhibit. (3) Deck-merge drill: four people's slides into one consistent deck in 20 minutes. (4) Appendix navigation: any called number on screen within five seconds. (5) Full AI-operator run of the SOP on a past case. (6) Compliance-check speed run.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Research rabbit holes.** → Every sprint is timeboxed at 15 minutes; the strategist can call "park it".
- **Deck-merge crunch at the end.** → Rolling merge from the start of P4; slide owners deliver inside the template, never in loose files.
- **An unsourced factoid reaches a slide.** → The source log is mandatory; the verification rules below are enforced at P5 — no exceptions for "I saw it somewhere".
- **Tech failure at handover.** → Save to USB early, verify it opens, keep a second copy; know the fixed pickup time cold; never rename files after the compliance check.

## 4. General-business strategist

**Mission.** Own the argument and the clock. Integrate the three specialists into one storyline covering every rubric row — above all *Implementation & resources* (2.5×, the single heaviest category), plus *Framing the problem*, *Impact*, and *Measurement, risks & mitigation*. Timekeeper during prep, marshal during Q&A.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| Prepared (25 %) | Problem framing and storyboard; executive summary; implementation roadmap with owners, timeline, resources; risk and KPI slide; Q&A grid; speaker allocation (every member must speak) |
| Boardroom (30 %) | The one-page executive brief (drafting owner); protected-3-minute script; summary-slide layout; risk/measurement appendix; discussion battle plan assigning question themes so every member speaks |
| Traditional (45 %) | Framing slide; storyline; implementation plan; KPI/risk slide; Q&A grid; timing plan for 20 + 10 |
| Final | Q&A grid v2; timing plan for the 20 + 15 format with a fresh judge panel |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | Traditional (3 h) | Boardroom (4 h) |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Read top-down for the decision-maker's actual question; draft problem statement and success criteria | Same |
| P2 | Chair the timeboxed direction decision; lock the one-sentence recommendation; assign slide ownership; post the clock plan | Same, plus sketch the brief skeleton |
| P3 | Build the implementation skeleton (phases, owners, resources, timeline) in parallel; mid-sprint thesis check at halfway | Same, with more depth on resourcing and sequencing |
| P4 | Write the exec summary; sequence the deck into the storyline; draft the KPI/risk slide; enforce gate times | Finish the one-page brief early — judges read it before we enter — then the 3-minute script |
| P5 | Run the rehearsal: opening, transitions, card discipline; allocate Q&A themes; run the 10-minute AI routine with the CS lead | Drill the protected 3 minutes to ≤2:45 against the 1-minute red card; then a mock stretch of the discussion |
| P6 | Final storyline read; confirm every rubric row has a home slide | Same, brief included |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) Weekly 3-minute pitch on one summary slide. (2) Brief-writing drill: one black-and-white page from a past case in 40 minutes. (3) Implementation-plan drill: any recommendation to a resourced 90-day/12-month plan in 20 minutes. (4) Q&A marshaling against a coach-led panel. (5) Timekeeping a full mock under the card protocol.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Storyline as afterthought.** → Paper storyboard exists by the end of P2; slides fit the story, never the reverse.
- **Thin implementation slide in the 2.5× category.** → Hard minimum: phases, owners, budget line from finance, timeline, first-90-days detail, KPIs.
- **Running over time.** → Rehearse to 90 % of allotted time; when tight, the strategist cuts content — nobody speeds up delivery.
- **Q&A chaos.** → Marshal protocol: each question is routed to exactly one owner; others add only on a hand signal.

## GenAI standard operating procedure

The rules explicitly allow internet during timed prep, including university databases, file sharing and generative AI. Our stance: AI is a fifth analyst with zero accountability — fast, occasionally wrong, and never the one answering judges. It checks, stresses and polishes our work; it never does our thinking.

**Operating lanes — who prompts, for what.** Default is a **single-operator model**: the CS & analytics lead runs the team's AI lane, so we never have four chat sessions producing conflicting output. Three narrow personal lanes exist:

- **Finance lead:** arithmetic and formula verification of the finance lead's own model outputs ("recompute this payback from these inputs").
- **Innovation lead:** counterargument generation against the chosen concept, in P5 only.
- **Strategist:** wording polish for the executive summary and one-page brief in P4, and the Q&A-anticipation routine in P5.

**Sanctioned tasks:** checking math; stress-testing the recommendation ("argue against this as a skeptical CFO"); generating counterarguments and likely judge questions; polishing exec-summary and headline wording; condensing text to fit the one-page brief; scouting public benchmarks *for verification at source*; formatting clean-up. **Never outsourced:** the problem framing, the option choice, and the core recommendation are made by the four of us. If an AI critique makes us want to change course, that is a team decision the strategist chairs — AI input is one voice, not a verdict.

**Verification rules — no unverified number reaches a slide.**

1. Every externally sourced figure needs a primary source in the source log (case exhibit, filing, official statistic, reputable database). An AI answer is never a source and is never cited.
2. Finance independently recomputes any AI-checked math once; on disagreement, finance's own calculation stands pending a source check.
3. External benchmarks need two independent sources, or they are relabeled as assumptions with a stated basis.
4. After the P6 number freeze, no new AI-derived content of any kind enters deck, brief or talk track.
5. Anything that cannot be verified in the time available becomes a clearly labeled assumption — or it is cut.

**Confidentiality cautions.** The cases are confidential: no reproduction, sharing, or discussion outside the competition context. Therefore: never paste the case PDF, its exhibits, or long verbatim extracts into consumer AI tools — assume anything pasted may be retained. Prefer university-licensed or enterprise tools with contractual no-training/zero-retention terms, configured and login-tested before we fly. Prompt by paraphrase and abstraction — our own words, minimum necessary detail ("a regional credit union wants members under 30"), never client-identifiable case language. Keep our school name out of every prompt and visible screen (anonymity is a disqualification rule), and never use any tool to contact anyone — outside contact is banned after release. Before February: **read the 2027 event guide for any new AI clauses** (the 2025 baseline simply allows GenAI) and ask the organizers (aspercasecomps@umanitoba.ca) whether 2027 adds tool restrictions, disclosure duties, or AI-specific confidentiality rules — this sits on our open-questions list.

**The 10-minute AI-assisted Q&A-anticipation routine** (P5; strategist + CS lead at one screen):

| Minutes | Step |
|---|---|
| 0:00–2:00 | Strategist dictates a paraphrased brief — recommendation, three key numbers, main risk. No case text is pasted. |
| 2:00–4:30 | Prompt 1: "You are a skeptical judging panel (CFO, COO, marketing executive). Ask the 15 hardest questions about this recommendation, ordered by likelihood." Steer toward implementation and resources first — the heaviest rubric line. |
| 4:30–6:00 | Prompt 2: "Which three of these would this team struggle with most, and why?" |
| 6:00–8:30 | Team huddle: every question gets an owner and a one-line answer on the Q&A grid; finance verifies any number used in an answer. |
| 8:30–10:00 | Strategist scripts bridges for the two weakest spots ("we would validate that in the pilot's first 30 days — the decision does not hinge on it"), then closes the lane. AI output never appears on a slide. |
