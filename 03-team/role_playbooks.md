# ABCC 2027 Role Playbooks — Team HSBI

**Audience:** the four HSBI competitors and the coach. **Scope:** who does what, minute by minute, in the timed rounds, plus our generative-AI operating procedure and the prep-room kit. Facts follow `01-competition/facts_and_rules.md`: the published 2027 schedule where it speaks, the 2025 Event Guide baseline where it is silent. **Updated 2026-10-31 for the 10-hour third round** — every minute-map now carries a 10-hour column.

**Context in one paragraph.** The fifth annual ABCC runs Mon 15 – Sat 20 February 2027 at the Drake Centre, Asper School of Business, Winnipeg — nine teams of four. Three scored rounds: the **prepared case** (25 %; 25-minute presentation + 15-minute Q&A, submitted as .pptx by e-mail; late submissions go to the bottom of the pool), the **boardroom case** (30 %; 4-hour on-site prep, then a **protected 3-minute recommendation on one summary slide** with appendices blocked, followed by a **22-minute discussion** with appendices accessible; judges receive our **one-page black-and-white executive brief** in advance), and the **third round** (45 %; 20-minute presentation + 10-minute Q&A) — a 3-hour **traditional case** in 2025 and 2026, listed for 2027 as a **10-hour case**: a full prep day on Fri 19 February, presentations on Sat 20 February (working assumption until the organizers confirm; `01-competition/facts_and_rules.md`, open question 1). The top three reach a final with **frozen decks** (20 + 15) the same Saturday; ties break on the higher third-round score — **the 10-hour round is where the competition is won**, and it comes at the end of a five-day week, after the boardroom (Wed) and an excursion day (Thu). Every member must speak in every round (in the boardroom, during the discussion). During on-site prep: internet, university databases, file sharing and **generative AI are allowed**; one laptop per student; no phones or smartwatches; no coach or outside contact after release; identifying our school to a judge means disqualification. Timing cards: yellow at 5 minutes, red at 1.

## The shared team clock

All four playbooks run on the same six phases so hand-offs are predictable. Slots are staggered and **late arrival earns no extra time**; the clock starts the second the case is handed over.

| Phase | 3 h (2025/2026 traditional; speed runs) | Boardroom (4 h) | **10-hour case (2027)** | Team output at gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 Read & frame | 0:00–0:20 | 0:00–0:25 | 0:00–0:45 | Everyone has read; key facts logged |
| P2 Align & decide | 0:20–0:40 | 0:25–0:55 | 0:45–1:30 | One-sentence recommendation locked (10 h: *provisional*, plus a research plan) |
| P3 Analysis sprint | 0:40–1:40 | 0:55–2:15 | 1:30–6:00 (incl. Gate A + lunch) | Model, evidence, concept detail done |
| P4 Build | 1:40–2:25 | 2:15–3:15 | 6:00–8:00 (Gate B at 6:00) | Deck (+ brief) assembled in template |
| P5 Verify & rehearse | 2:25–2:50 | 3:15–3:45 | 8:00–9:30 (freeze at 8:30) | Numbers audited; Q&A grid filled; full run-through |
| P6 Lockdown & USB | 2:50–3:00 | 3:45–4:00 | 9:30–9:45, then 15 min buffer | Files named, saved to provided USB, verified |

Two hard gates: the direction locked at P2 is reopened only by the strategist (at an announced 10-minute cost), and a **number freeze** starts at P6 — after it, nothing new goes on a slide.

### The 10-hour day (2027 third round)

Ten hours is not a slow three-hour round. Judges will expect what the extra seven hours can buy — real desk research, a tested model with sensitivities, a costed implementation plan with a first-90-days view — and the failure mode changes from *running out of time* to *running out of energy and discipline*. The clock below assumes a 08:30 release; shift every time by the actual release. If the organizers confirm a shorter round, the 3-hour column above is the fallback and stays drilled (W14 speed run).

| Clock | Block | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:45 | **P1 Read & frame** — everyone reads the full case; key facts, exhibits and the decision-maker's question logged | No laptops for research until 0:30 |
| 0:45–1:30 | **P2 Align & decide** — option stubs, kill, *provisional* recommendation; **research plan**: the 5–8 questions whose answers could change the decision, each with an owner | The plan is written; research outside it needs the strategist's nod |
| 1:30–3:30 | **P3a Research & first analysis** — GenAI-assisted desk research (CS lead's lane, per the SOP), benchmarks into the source log, first model pass, concept detail | Every research thread timeboxed at 30 min |
| 3:30–3:50 | **Gate A — direction confirmed.** The only point where the recommendation may change without the 10-minute penalty; paper storyboard of the deck agreed | After Gate A the P2 rule applies again |
| 3:50–4:20 | **Lunch** — all four, away from screens | Non-negotiable; the clock-keeper calls it |
| 4:20–6:00 | **P3b Deep analysis** — full model with sensitivities, implementation plan (phases, owners, budget, timeline, first 90 days), KPI and risk set, alternatives considered | Finance pushes interim outputs every 30 min |
| 6:00 | **Gate B — storyline frozen.** Every slide has its action title; ghost deck in the template; speaker allocation set | New slides after Gate B only by strategist decision |
| 6:00–8:00 | **P4 Build** — rolling merge from minute one; 15-minute break with food around 7:00 | Appendix bank built in parallel for Q&A |
| 8:00–8:30 | **P5a Number audit** — finance recomputes every displayed figure; compliance check | — |
| 8:30 | **Content and number freeze** | Only typos, layout and slide order after this |
| 8:30–9:15 | **P5b Full timed rehearsal** — 20 + 10 with cards, one member playing judge | Cut content if over 18 minutes; nobody speeds up |
| 9:15–9:30 | **10-minute AI Q&A-anticipation routine** + Q&A grid owners | Answers go on the grid, never on a slide |
| 9:30–9:45 | **P6 Lockdown & USB** — exact file name, save, open from the stick, back up | — |
| 9:45–10:00 | **Buffer** — absorbs one crisis; if unused, a second read of the Q&A grid | Never spent on new content |

**Energy management.** The 10-hour day comes on Friday of a long week; the boardroom is two days earlier and presentations follow on Saturday morning. Water and food on the table all day, a stand-up minute every hour, and a hard stop for lunch. The strategist runs the clock but the CS lead calls the breaks, so the person most absorbed in the argument is not the one deciding to rest. After lockdown: dinner, one read of the Q&A grid, sleep — no deck work overnight (the deck is handed in at the end of prep; confirm the exact hand-in with the organizers).

## 1. Finance lead

**Mission.** Own every number the team shows or says. The heaviest rubric line, *Implementation & resources* (2.5×), explicitly asks for financial and resource considerations, and *Analysis* (2.0×) demands quantitative substantiation — so this role converts every idea into costs, benefits, payback and a funded plan. Nothing financial reaches a slide unless this role produced or verified it.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared (25 %) | Full financial model workbook; impact exhibit (payback/NPV); costed implementation budget; sensitivity table |
| Boardroom (30 %) | The three headline figures on the single summary slide; financial paragraph of the one-page brief; 3–5 financial appendix slides; costed resourcing plan |
| Third round (45 %; 10-hour case in 2027) | Quantified-impact slide; implementation budget and resourcing slide; financial appendices; assumptions register |
| Final | Nothing new (decks frozen) — total command of every number for the 15-minute Q&A |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | 3 h (2025/2026 traditional; speed runs) | Boardroom (4 h) | 10-hour case (2027) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Read financial exhibits first; log every given figure into the assumptions register | Same, with 5 extra minutes on the client's baseline economics | 10-hour: Same, plus rebuild the client's baseline economics from the exhibits before anyone proposes an option |
| P2 | Two-minute "size of the prize" estimate per option to inform the vote | Same; flag which option we can credibly cost in 4 hours | Size of the prize per option; name the 2–3 inputs that need external data and put them on the research plan |
| P3 | Build the model from the prebuilt shell: baseline, option economics, implementation costs; push interim outputs to the strategist | Same, deeper: add sensitivity and a funding/resourcing view for the brief | P3a: model v1 from the shell by Gate A. P3b: full model — scenarios, sensitivity, funding and resourcing, multi-year view; interim outputs every 30 min |
| P4 | Turn the model into 2–3 slides (impact, budget, sensitivity); supply exec-summary numbers | Deliver max three headline figures for the summary slide; write the brief's financial lines; build the financial appendix | 3–4 slides (impact, budget, sensitivity, funding) plus a deep financial appendix bank for Q&A |
| P5 | **Number audit:** recompute every displayed figure from source; run the AI math-check lane; brief the team on the three numbers judges will attack | Same, plus rehearse a "walk me through your model" appendix path for the discussion | Number audit 8:00–8:30 before the freeze; then rehearse "walk me through your model" answers |
| P6 | Freeze the assumptions register; tie out final deck against the workbook | Same, plus confirm brief tables read in black-and-white print | Same as 3 h; keep the workbook open offline for the Saturday morning re-read |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) Model-shell speed drill: blank prebuilt shell to working option economics in 45 minutes. (2) Mental-math and clean-rounding drill for live Q&A. (3) Rebuild the numbers for a past case (e.g., Access Credit Union 2026) end-to-end. (4) Sensitivity one-pager in 10 minutes. (5) Coach-led assumption gauntlet: defend every input for 15 minutes.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Over-modeling.** The model becomes an end in itself. → Hard gate: slide-ready outputs by end of P3; the strategist may call "good enough" and it stands.
- **Orphan numbers.** Slide edits drift from the model. → One workbook is the single source of truth; number freeze at P6 with a final tie-out.
- **False precision.** "€1,247,332" invites attack. → Round aggressively, show ranges, label assumptions.
- **Freezing on an assumption challenge.** → The assumptions register carries a rationale and a sensitivity for each input: "at half our uptake, payback moves from 18 to 30 months — the recommendation holds."

## 2. Innovation lead

**Mission.** Own the distinctiveness and concreteness of the recommendation. *Recommendation* weighs 2.0× and nine teams see the same case; recent clients explicitly wanted boldness (Jazz Winnipeg: a "radical new operational idea"; Coal & Canary: premium to luxury; Access Credit Union: youth-engaging service innovations). Generate wide, kill fast, make the survivor vivid and implementable.

**Owned artifacts per round.**

| Round | Artifacts |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared (25 %) | Option tree with kill log; concept slides (what it is, why this client, why now); differentiation story; pilot design |
| Boardroom (30 %) | The recommendation's core wording and label on the summary slide; counterargument prep sheet for the discussion; concept appendix |
| Third round (45 %; 10-hour case in 2027) | Concept and "why this wins" slide; alternatives-considered slide; pilot/rollout design input |
| Final | Sharpened articulation; rehearsed answers to every "why not X?" |

**Minute-map.**

| Phase | 3 h (2025/2026 traditional; speed runs) | Boardroom (4 h) | 10-hour case (2027) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | Read for customer, market and constraint signals; draft 5–7 option stubs | Same, plus note what the client tried before (recent boardroom panels included a company representative) | Same, plus a list of precedents worth researching in P3a |
| P2 | Pitch each stub in 30 seconds; run the kill; lock direction with the strategist | Same, plus draft the one-sentence recommendation for the protected 3 minutes | Same, but the lock is provisional; write the research questions that could kill or confirm the leading idea |
| P3 | Make the idea concrete: features, customer journey, differentiation; pull precedent with the CS lead; feed sizing inputs to finance | Same, deeper: define the pilot and the "day one" experience | P3a: precedent and customer research with the CS lead; bring the confirm/kill evidence to Gate A. P3b: full concept, customer journey, pilot design, differentiation check |
| P4 | Build concept and alternatives-considered slides (considered alternatives score under *Analysis*) | Own the recommendation wording on the summary slide; build the concept appendix | Concept, alternatives-considered and pilot slides; concept appendix |
| P5 | Red-team own idea via the AI counterargument lane; script "why not the obvious alternative?" answers | Same, tuned for the interactive 22-minute discussion | Same as 3 h, after the freeze — counterarguments feed the Q&A grid, not the deck |
| P6 | Final coherence read: does every slide serve the idea's story? | Same | Same |

**Pre-competition drills.** (1) Ten ideas in ten minutes, weekly. (2) Kill-criteria drill: impact/feasibility/fit screen in 5 minutes. (3) Analogy transfer: adapt a proven model from an adjacent industry in 15 minutes. (4) "So what?" chains: any feature to client benefit in three steps. (5) Regenerate options for past ABCC cases; compare against what strong teams chose.

**Failure modes and countermeasures.**

- **Falling in love with idea #1.** → Minimum five options and a written kill log before P2 lock — no exceptions.
- **Clever but unimplementable.** The heaviest rubric line is implementation (2.5×). → Every concept element must map to a resourced step in the strategist's plan; unmappable elements are cut or parked as "phase 2".
- **Reopening the decision mid-build.** → Only the strategist can reopen, at an announced 10-minute cost.
- **A generic answer eight other teams will give.** → P3 differentiation check: "Which two teams will say exactly this — and what is our twist?"

- **An unsourced factoid reaches a slide.** → The source log is mandatory; the verification rules below are enforced at P5 — no exceptions for "I saw it somewhere".
- **Tech failure at handover.** → Save to USB early, verify it opens, keep a second copy; know the fixed pickup time cold; never rename files after the compliance check.

- **Running over time.** → Rehearse to 90 % of allotted time; when tight, the strategist cuts content — nobody speeds up delivery.
- **Q&A chaos.** → Marshal protocol: each question is routed to exactly one owner; others add only on a hand signal.

## GenAI standard operating procedure

The rules explicitly allow internet during timed prep, including university databases, file sharing and generative AI. Our stance: AI is a fifth analyst with zero accountability — fast, occasionally wrong, and never the one answering judges. It checks, stresses and polishes our work; it never does our thinking.

**Operating lanes — who prompts, for what.** Default is a **single-operator model**: the CS & analytics lead runs the team's AI lane, so we never have four chat sessions producing conflicting output. Three narrow personal lanes exist:

- **Finance lead:** arithmetic and formula verification of the finance lead's own model outputs ("recompute this payback from these inputs").
- **Innovation lead:** counterargument generation against the chosen concept, in P5 only.
- **Strategist:** wording polish for the executive summary and one-page brief in P4, and the Q&A-anticipation routine in P5.

**Sanctioned tasks:** checking math; stress-testing the recommendation ("argue against this as a skeptical CFO"); generating counterarguments and likely judge questions; polishing exec-summary and headline wording; condensing text to fit the one-page brief; scouting public benchmarks *for verification at source*; formatting clean-up. **Never outsourced:** the problem framing, the option choice, and the core recommendation are made by the four of us. If an AI critique makes us want to change course, that is a team decision the strategist chairs — AI input is one voice, not a verdict.

**Verification rules — no unverified number reaches a slide.**

1. Every externally sourced figure needs a primary source in the source log (case exhibit, filing, official statistic, reputable database). An AI answer is never a source and is never cited.
2. Finance independently recomputes any AI-checked math once; on disagreement, finance's own calculation stands pending a source check.
3. External benchmarks need two independent sources, or they are relabeled as assumptions with a stated basis.
4. After the P6 number freeze, no new AI-derived content of any kind enters deck, brief or talk track.
5. Anything that cannot be verified in the time available becomes a clearly labeled assumption — or it is cut.

**Confidentiality cautions.** The cases are confidential: no reproduction, sharing, or discussion outside the competition context. Therefore: never paste the case PDF, its exhibits, or long verbatim extracts into consumer AI tools — assume anything pasted may be retained. Prefer university-licensed or enterprise tools with contractual no-training/zero-retention terms, configured and login-tested before we fly. Prompt by paraphrase and abstraction — our own words, minimum necessary detail ("a regional credit union wants members under 30"), never client-identifiable case language. Keep our school name out of every prompt and visible screen (anonymity is a disqualification rule), and never use any tool to contact anyone — outside contact is banned after release. Before February: **read the 2027 event guide for any new AI clauses** (the 2025 baseline simply allows GenAI) and ask the organizers (aspercasecomps@umanitoba.ca) whether 2027 adds tool restrictions, disclosure duties, or AI-specific confidentiality rules — this sits on our open-questions list.

**The 10-minute AI-assisted Q&A-anticipation routine** (P5; strategist + CS lead at one screen):

| Minutes | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–2:00 | Strategist dictates a paraphrased brief — recommendation, three key numbers, main risk. No case text is pasted. |
| 2:00–4:30 | Prompt 1: "You are a skeptical judging panel (CFO, COO, marketing executive). Ask the 15 hardest questions about this recommendation, ordered by likelihood." Steer toward implementation and resources first — the heaviest rubric line. |
| 4:30–6:00 | Prompt 2: "Which three of these would this team struggle with most, and why?" |
| 6:00–8:30 | Team huddle: every question gets an owner and a one-line answer on the Q&A grid; finance verifies any number used in an answer. |
| 8:30–10:00 | Strategist scripts bridges for the two weakest spots ("we would validate that in the pilot's first 30 days — the decision does not hinge on it"), then closes the lane. AI output never appears on a slide. |
