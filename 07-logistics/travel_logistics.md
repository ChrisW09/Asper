## 1. Entry requirements: eTA for German citizens

German citizens are **visa-exempt** for Canada but need an **Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA)** to board a flight to Canada.

- **Apply only at the official site:** canada.ca/eta (IRCC). Third-party sites charge inflated fees for the same form.
- **Cost:** CAD 7 per person, paid online by credit/debit card.
- **What you need:** valid German passport, credit card, e-mail address; the form takes about 10 minutes and asks a few background questions.
- **Processing:** most applications are approved **within minutes** by e-mail, but some are flagged for document review and can take **several days** — apply as soon as passports are confirmed, not the week before departure.
- **Validity:** up to **5 years or until the passport expires**, whichever comes first. The eTA is **electronically linked to the passport number** used in the application — enter it carefully, and travel on exactly that passport. A new passport means a new eTA.
- **Passports:** must be valid for the intended stay; in practice aim for **6+ months validity beyond February 2027**. Have all five team members verify passports before booking flights (names on tickets must match passports exactly).
- An eTA does not guarantee entry; the border officer at first arrival in Canada makes the final decision. Carry the competition invitation letter and hotel confirmation in hand luggage.

**Note on US transit:** some cheap itineraries route via Minneapolis (MSP), Chicago (ORD) or Denver. Transiting the USA requires an **ESTA (currently USD 40)** and a full US immigration entry — avoidable hassle. **Route via Canada** and only an eTA is needed.

## 2. Flights: Germany → Winnipeg (YWG)

There are **no nonstop flights from Germany to Winnipeg**; plan one connection inside Canada.

**Getting to the departure airport from Bielefeld:** ICE from Bielefeld Hbf to **Frankfurt (FRA)** takes roughly 3–3.5 hours (Düsseldorf ~2 h is an alternative gateway). Lufthansa's **Rail&Fly** ticket protects the rail leg as part of the itinerary.

**Typical routings (one stop):**

| Routing | Carriers | Approx. times |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FRA → Toronto (YYZ) → YWG | Air Canada / Lufthansa + Air Canada, WestJet or Porter | FRA–YYZ ~8.5 h; YYZ–YWG ~2 h 50 min, many daily departures |
| FRA → Montreal (YUL) → YWG | Lufthansa / Air Canada | similar total, fewer onward frequencies |
| FRA or MUC → Calgary/Vancouver → YWG | Lufthansa + WestJet/Air Canada | backtracking eastward on the last leg |
| AMS → Calgary or Toronto → YWG | KLM + WestJet/connecting carrier | option if AMS fares are better; add rail to Amsterdam |

The Toronto routing is the workhorse: door-to-door about **14–18 hours**. With the 7-hour time shift, a morning departure from Frankfurt lands you in Winnipeg by **evening the same day**.

**Prices (economy return, February = low season):** aggregator data shows Frankfurt–Winnipeg one-ways from roughly **US$370**; realistic return fares are about **€650–1,100 per person** including a checked bag, with Lufthansa/Air Canada often €900–1,200 if booked late. Budget ~**€1,000 per person** and book **3–6 months out** (i.e., by autumn 2026). Book all five travellers on the **same itinerary** and allow **minimum 2 hours** for the Toronto connection (you clear Canadian immigration and recheck bags at the first Canadian airport).

**Recommended arrival:** land **February 16 or 17** to absorb jet lag before the opening day on the 18th. Return flight on **February 22 or 23** (overnight eastbound, arriving Germany a day later).

## 3. Delta Hotels Winnipeg (team hotel)

The competition houses visiting teams at **Delta Hotels Winnipeg** (Marriott Bonvoy), **350 St. Mary Avenue, Winnipeg, MB R3C 3J2**, in the heart of downtown.

- **Skywalk connected:** direct indoor access to the city's skywalk system, the **RBC Convention Centre** and **Canada Life Centre** (Winnipeg Jets arena; older sources say "MTS Centre") — in February you can reach restaurants and shops without going outside.
- **Amenities:** indoor pool plus a heated **rooftop outdoor pool**, Odyssey Fitness Centre, **Blaze Restaurant & Lounge**, and the Urban Bean coffeehouse (serving Starbucks) for pre-round caffeine.
- **Booking:** the ABCC organizers typically arrange the room block — confirm with aspercasecomps@umanitoba.ca whether rooms are billed to teams and reserve early. Ask about a quiet room block; teams practice in rooms the night before rounds.
- ~10 km / 15 minutes by taxi from the airport.

## 4. Airport → downtown

Winnipeg's airport is **Winnipeg James Armstrong Richardson International (YWG)**, unusually close to the city (~10 km).

- **Taxi:** ~**CAD 20–30**, about **15 minutes** to the Delta. For 5 people plus luggage and a laptop bag each, take **two taxis** or request a van at the rank.
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber operates in Winnipeg at similar prices.
- **Public bus:** Winnipeg Transit routes serve the airport (adult cash fare ~**CAD 3.35–3.60**, ~40 min) — fine for a solo traveller, impractical for a team with suitcases at –20 °C.

## 5. Campus, Drake Centre and daily transfers

The competition runs at the **Drake Centre** on the University of Manitoba's **Fort Garry campus**, about **11 km south of downtown** along the Red River — it is *not* walkable from the hotel. Options:

- **Organizer shuttles:** host schools usually shuttle teams between the Delta and campus — confirm the 2027 schedule with the coordinators and plan around it.

The Drake Centre houses the Asper School of Business; competition rounds (prepared case, boardroom case, traditional case) run there, so treat it as your daytime base.

## 6. February weather and clothing — take this seriously

Winnipeg is one of the coldest major cities on Earth in winter. February averages: **high ≈ −9 °C, low ≈ −20 °C**, with cold snaps below **−30 °C** and wind chills that can feel like **−40**. Snow cover is constant but new snowfall is light (dry prairie cold); daylight is decent (~10 h, roughly 07:40–17:45).

**Required kit per person:**

- Insulated winter parka rated to −30 °C (hood essential)
- Warm hat covering the ears, scarf or neck gaiter, **insulated gloves or mittens**
- Thermal base layers (top and bottom) for any outdoor walking
- **Insulated, waterproof winter boots with grip** — pavements are icy; leather dress shoes outdoors are dangerous and will be ruined by road salt
- Wear boots in transit and **carry business shoes and suits in a garment bag**; everyone changes indoors. Lip balm and moisturizer help with the extremely dry air.

Downtown's skywalk network and the hotel's indoor links mean daily exposure can be minutes, not hours — but never underestimate the walk between a bus and a door.

## 7. Money, tipping, SIM

- **Currency:** Canadian dollar (CAD). Cards and contactless (Visa/Mastercard, Apple/Google Pay) work almost everywhere; carry only ~CAD 50–100 cash per person. Always choose to pay **in CAD**, never the terminal's euro conversion (DCC). Notify German banks of travel.
- **Taxes:** displayed prices exclude tax — Manitoba adds **5% GST + 7% PST (~12%)** at the till.
- **Tipping is expected:** **15–20%** in restaurants (on the pre-tax amount), ~10–15% for taxis, CAD 1–2 per drink at bars/coffee counters, CAD 2–5 for hotel housekeeping.
- **Connectivity:** EU roaming rules do **not** apply in Canada and German-provider roaming is expensive. Best options: a **travel eSIM** (e.g., Airalo/Holafly-type, ~€15–30 for 10–20 GB Canada) bought before departure, or a Canadian prepaid SIM (Lucky Mobile, Chatr, Public Mobile, ~CAD 30–45/month). The University of Manitoba supports **eduroam** — university credentials should work on campus — and the hotel has Wi-Fi.

## 8. Travel insurance

- German statutory health insurance (**GKV**) provides **no coverage in Canada**, and Canadian care for visitors is expensive (an ER visit can exceed CAD 1,000; hospitalization far more). **Auslandsreisekrankenversicherung is mandatory for every team member** — annual policies (HanseMerkur, ADAC, TravelSecure, etc.) cost roughly €10–25 and must include **medical repatriation** ("medizinisch sinnvoller Rücktransport").
- Check whether the university's **Dienstreise/group travel policy** covers the professor and enrolled students — get written confirmation of who is covered for what.
- Given non-refundable fares booked months ahead, **trip cancellation insurance (Reiserücktritt)** for ~€3,500–5,000 total trip cost is sensible; baggage insurance is optional.

## 9. Jet lag strategy (7 hours)

Winnipeg is on **Central Standard Time (UTC−6)** in February; Germany is on CET (UTC+1) — Winnipeg is **7 hours behind Bielefeld**.

- **Before departure:** for 2–3 nights, go to bed and wake **1–2 hours later** than usual.
- **Flight day:** set watches to Winnipeg time at boarding; nap briefly (<1 h) early in the flight, then stay awake.
- **On arrival (evening local):** stay up until 21:30–22:00 local; avoid a long first-night lie-in.
- **First days:** seek **morning daylight**, keep caffeine before ~13:00 local, optional low-dose melatonin at local bedtime for the first 2–3 nights. Westbound adaptation is the easy direction — expect to be functional by day 2 and sharp by the first competition round if you arrive Feb 16/17. Morning presentations at 09:00 feel like 16:00 body time, which is favorable.
- **Return:** eastbound is harder — plan a light schedule for 3–4 days after arriving back and avoid teaching/exams the day after landing.
