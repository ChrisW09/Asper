# Case-Type Playbook — How to Tackle the Six ABCC Case Types

Companion to the six PowerPoint skeletons in `06-templates/case-type-templates/`. Each chapter tells you how to **recognize** a type in the first ten minutes, the **core question** judges want answered, the **analyses that decide it**, the **storyline** (mirroring the template slide by slide), the **judge attacks** to expect, and the **traps** that sink teams. The generic all-purpose skeleton remains `06-templates/team_template.pptx`; these six are pre-bent toward a case type so the storyline pass takes minutes, not an hour.

The types are induced from the actual ABCC client formula (see `01-competition/past_cases.md`, Part III): the prepared round has been an arts/culture non-profit three years running, the boardroom round an owner-led consumer or family business at a crossroads, the traditional round a large Manitoba-rooted enterprise with an engagement, trust, or talent problem.

## The first ten minutes: classifying the case

Read the ask twice before anything else. The task sentence almost always names the type: *viability/radical idea* → Turnaround (A); *expansion/growth/pilot budget* → Growth & Entry (B); *positioning/brand elevation* → Premiumization (C); *next product/opportunity, with numeric screens* → Opportunity Screen (D); *engage segment X / service innovation / digital* → Engagement (E); *hiring/trust/adoption/change* → People & Change (F). Then confirm against the client archetype and exhibits: a deficit P&L signals A; a market/competitor table signals B or D; brand-tracking data signals C; funnel or demographic data signals E; workforce or adoption data signals F.

Two rules govern hybrids. First, **the ask wins**: a bank case with brand-tracking exhibits but an ask about young customers is Type E, and the brand data becomes evidence inside the E storyline. Second, **borrow slides, not storylines**: take a single analysis slide from a second type's template if an exhibit demands it, but never merge two storylines — a 20-minute deck carries exactly one narrative arc.

Whatever the type, three constants from the official rubric apply: Implementation & Resources is the heaviest criterion (2.5×), so the roadmap slide is never the one you cut; Analysis (2.0×) means every claim traces to an exhibit or a stated assumption; and Measurement/Risks (1.0×) is cheap insurance — a risk table and KPI strip take ten minutes and are pre-built in every template.

## Type A — Turnaround & Revenue Sustainability

**Template:** `casetype_A_turnaround_nonprofit.pptx` · **ABCC precedents:** WAG-Qaumajuq 2024, Manitoba Museum 2025, Jazz Winnipeg 2026 (all prepared round)

**Recognize it:** a non-profit or arts organization, a structural deficit, flat or shrinking grants, an aging audience, reserves with a countdown, and an ask that says "sustainable," "viable," or "radical new idea."

**Core question:** which *one* signature move closes the structural gap within the organization's real capacity — without damaging the mission that legitimizes its grants and sponsors?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) a deficit bridge showing which P&L lines leak and which could grow; (2) a revenue-mix benchmark against two or three named peer organizations that solved the same problem; (3) an option screen scoring financial impact × mission fit × execution capacity, with rejected options shown; (4) a break-even model of the signature move with sensitivity.

**Storyline:** burning platform (gap, runway, dependency) → where the money leaks → what peers did → option screen → the signature move in depth → the financial bridge to surplus → stakeholder management → roadmap → risks and the ask.

**Judge attacks:** "Your move dilutes the mission" (answer: mission-fit column in the screen, artistic sovereignty explicitly protected); "A three-person team cannot execute this" (answer: capacity column, phasing, partners); "What if demand doesn't come?" (answer: sensitivity plus the pilot gate); "Why not just cut costs?" (answer: the rejected option row — cutting the programme cuts the reason funders pay).

**Traps:** the laundry list (ten small ideas, none funded); recommending what the client already tried (read the case history); treating grants as guaranteed; forgetting that in arts organizations the *audience* and the *funders* are different customers with different value propositions.

**Self-check before freeze:** Can the treasurer photograph one slide with the full bridge from −€X to +€Y? Is one rejected option visible with its reason? Does the roadmap fit the organization's staffing reality?

## Type B — Growth & Market Entry

**Template:** `casetype_B_growth_market_entry.pptx` · **ABCC precedent:** Independent Jewellers 2024 (boardroom)

**Recognize it:** an owner-led or family firm, a saturated or small home market, growth ambition, often an explicit pilot budget, and constraints such as brand-partner rules or limited capital.

**Core question:** where to play and how to enter — and which single gated pilot buys the most learning per euro before anything irreversible is committed?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) a market screen over three to five candidates on attractiveness *and* right-to-win; (2) a transferable-advantage test — what actually travels beyond the founder's home market; (3) entry-mode economics (own site vs digital-first vs partner vs pop-up); (4) a pilot design with budget, KPIs, and an explicit go/no-go gate.

**Storyline:** why growth must come from outside (saturation + existing external demand) → market screen → what travels (and the binding constraint) → entry-mode comparison → unit economics and break-even volume → the gated pilot → roadmap with conditional phases → risks and a deliberately small ask.

**Judge attacks:** "Why this market and not X?" (the screen answers it — keep the also-rans in the appendix); "This cannibalizes your home store" (quantify the overlap; usually small); "Your marquee constraint blocks this" (address the constraint head-on — e.g., if the flagship brand cannot be sold online, the pilot sells what can be); "What kills the pilot?" (the gate criteria — state them before being asked).

**Traps:** size-only market ranking; "open stores" without unit economics; pretending the constraint away; a pilot without a kill criterion; asking the board for the full national rollout instead of the gate.

**Self-check:** Is the ask small enough to say yes to in a 22-minute discussion? Does every phase beyond the pilot carry an explicit condition?

## Type C — Brand Repositioning / Premiumization

**Template:** `casetype_C_brand_premiumization.pptx` · **ABCC precedent:** Coal & Canary 2026 (boardroom)

**Recognize it:** a consumer brand with craft or heritage credentials, a mid-tier price point, broad distribution, and founders who want to move upmarket.

**Core question:** full repositioning or two-tier architecture — and what happens to the existing channels and the cash engine while the brand climbs?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) the perception gap — what the brand-tracking data grants permission for and what it denies; (2) the luxury-codes checklist — scarcity, materials, story, service — and which the company can credibly adopt; (3) channel-consequence economics — what pruning wholesale and exiting marketplaces costs in contribution; (4) the transition P&L with a haircut applied to the client's own plan.

**Storyline:** the permission evidence → the codes we adopt (and refuse) → the two-tier architecture (core funds the climb, elevated line defines it) → channel hygiene with its price tag → the margin-mix bridge → launch sequence → roadmap → risks and the gated ask.

**Judge attacks:** "You'll alienate the loyal core customer" (the two-tier answer: the core line stays, at its price, for its customer); "Ubiquity killed your luxury story already" (the pruning plan is the answer — with the forgone contribution computed); "€95 candles from a €39 brand?" (pilot evidence: sell-out, waitlist, new-customer share); "Why will wholesale partners accept this?" (curated-door strategy plus elevated presentation for those who stay).

**Traps:** betting the company on the top tier; luxury-by-price-increase alone; ignoring the voucher/discount machinery that contradicts price integrity; scaling a scarce line until it stops being scarce.

**Self-check:** Does the deck state what you *refuse* to do? Is the cost of channel pruning quantified before a judge asks?

## Type D — New Product / Opportunity Screen

**Template:** `casetype_D_new_product_screen.pptx` · **ABCC precedent:** AROW Global 2025 (boardroom)

**Recognize it:** an industrial or B2B firm dominating a saturated niche, flat growth, and an ask with explicit numeric screens — revenue floor, margin floor, investment ceiling, payback window, no-go list.

**Core question:** which *one* adjacent opportunity passes every stated screen — and what is the cheapest staged validation before full commitment?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) a visible funnel from longlist to one winner, with the criteria that cut at each stage; (2) the screen tested number by number — every constraint from the case restated and passed; (3) a capability-transfer map (have / build / buy); (4) a gated validation plan with a stated maximum sunk cost.

**Storyline:** funnel → the constraint-compliance table (the centerpiece) → why us (capability transfer and right-to-win) → the market (who buys, who competes, why now) → validation gates and spend profile → roadmap → risks and a gate-1-only ask.

**Judge attacks:** every attack here is arithmetic. Judges recompute the compliance table live; one number that fails the screen under recomputation collapses the recommendation. Second wave: "Why not [runner-up]?" (funnel and appendix longlist); "The incumbent will respond" (fragmentation evidence, cost position); "Your margin assumption is optimistic" (bottom-up cost build in the appendix).

**Traps:** presenting a menu instead of a winner; violating an explicit no-go; a market sized only top-down; requesting the full investment with no gates; forgetting the case's *preferences* (they're tie-breakers judges care about).

**Self-check:** recompute every number in the compliance table from the exhibits, out loud, before freeze. State the maximum loss if gate 1 kills the idea.

## Type E — Digital Customer Engagement

**Template:** `casetype_E_digital_customer_engagement.pptx` · **ABCC precedent:** Access Credit Union 2026 (traditional); People Corp 2025 shares elements

**Recognize it:** a large incumbent (bank, credit union, insurer, utility, benefits platform) losing a demographic — usually the young — to digital-native rivals, with funnel or demographic exhibits.

**Core question:** which segment-led offer bundle wins the target group back, at an acquisition cost the lifetime value actually supports?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) sub-segmentation with jobs-to-be-done — students, young families, digital-native affluents are different customers; (2) funnel-leak analysis locating exactly where the target group is lost (usually onboarding completion and salary-account activation); (3) bundle design with *one* memorable hook that is structurally hard for the rival to copy (for a co-operative: real ownership economics); (4) CAC-vs-CLV cohort arithmetic with a payback year.

**Storyline:** the pipeline framing (today's young customer is tomorrow's mortgage) → the three sub-segments → the funnel leaks → the bundle and the hook → the CFO slide (CAC/CLV/payback) → the legacy base protected → phased roadmap → risks and ask.

**Judge attacks:** "Every bank says 'app plus youth account' — why is this different?" (the hook, and the segment sequencing); "You're abandoning your older base" (the AND-not-OR slide); "Gen Z doesn't care about your co-op identity" (concede the label, sell the substance: money back, real ownership, translated into their language); "CLV is speculative" (assumptions on-slide, sensitivity in appendix).

**Traps:** the generic app answer; averaging the segments; a hook the incumbent cannot deliver; spending without a payback horizon; ignoring that the branch network is both a cost problem and a trust asset.

**Self-check:** Can a judge repeat your hook in one sentence after the presentation? Does the funnel slide show *where* the money goes, not just that money is needed?

## Type F — Talent, Trust & Change

**Template:** `casetype_F_talent_and_change.pptx` · **ABCC precedents:** StandardAero 2024 (talent), People Corporation 2025 (trust), Manitoba Hydro 2023 (change-readiness)

**Recognize it:** a large enterprise whose binding constraint is human — unfilled skilled roles, internal distrust of a platform, change fatigue. The exhibits are workforce demographics, adoption curves, engagement surveys.

**Core question:** which segmented people-programme moves the few groups that actually matter — with measurable proof the organization can believe?

**The analyses that decide it:** (1) gap quantification and the cost of doing nothing (lost contribution, overtime premium, attrition cost — computable and devastating); (2) segmentation of the people (graduates, mid-career skeptics, near-retirement experts, works council) and what moves each; (3) programme portfolio with cost-per-net-outcome arithmetic, including one visibly rejected lever (the across-the-board raise that buys zero capacity); (4) leading indicators before lagging ones.

**Storyline:** the priced gap → its decomposition → the four audiences → the portfolio with €-per-outcome → the proof machine (trust is rebuilt by evidence, not messaging) → governance path (works council co-design) → roadmap with a visible 90-day win → risks and ask.

**Judge attacks:** "This is an HR brochure" (the arithmetic answers it — cost per net productive FTE); "The union will block it" (co-design from day one, on the main deck); "You're fixing the tool, not the trust" (the People-Corp lesson: dedicated support, guarantees, champions, relaunch); "How do we know it's working before year 2?" (leading indicators with monthly cadence).

**Traps:** generic employer branding; one message for all audiences; solving symptom instead of trust; programmes with no owner; ignoring German/unionized governance realities in cases that have them.

**Self-check:** Does something visible happen for employees within 90 days? Is the rejected lever's arithmetic on the slide?

## Using the templates

Duplicate the matching template at case release, delete its red briefing slide, and rename per the round's file convention ("[Team Name] <Round> Case"). The skeletons are storyline scaffolds: replace every bracketed placeholder, keep the action-title discipline (titles alone must tell the story), and re-check anonymity — no school name, logo, or city hint anywhere. Recolor navy/ice to the client's palette only if time remains after content is frozen. For the boardroom round, pair whichever type applies with the boardroom summary-slide layout in `06-templates/team_template.pptx` (slide 14) and the one-page brief template in `06-templates/judge_brief_template.docx`. Slide time follows the rubric: whatever the type, the implementation section is load-bearing (2.5×) — when in trouble, cut an analysis slide, never the roadmap.
