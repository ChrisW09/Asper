# Workbook 4 — Delivery: Pitch, Brief, Q&A Under Fire

Method references: `04-training/presentation_and_storyline_guide.md`, `04-training/qa_drills.md`, `04-training/english_phrasebook.md`. Everything spoken, everything timed, everything in English.

## Exercise 4.1 — The 3-minute pitch · weekly, rotate all four members

One member pitches from one summary slide, hard stop at 3:00 (red card shown at 2:00 → simulates the 1-minute warning). Scenarios: (1) FieldGuard to the AROW board (2) the under-30 bundle to the Sparkasse Vorstand (3) the two-tier line to Lichtwerk's founders (4) blockbusters-with-capped-downside to the Museum board (5) "Own It" youth platform to the Access CU board (6) free-admission funding model to the WAG board.

**Structure to hit:** 0:00–0:20 recommendation with headline number and date · 0:20–1:50 three pillars, each with its number · 1:50–2:30 the plan in three phases (2.5×!) · 2:30–3:00 top risk owned + the ask. Listener scores: recommendation inside 20 seconds? every pillar numbered? plan present? clean stop before the card?

> **Model pitch, scenario 1 (≈150 words, ~60 s/100 words pace).** "We recommend FieldGuard: entering operator-protection and cab-glazing for agricultural and construction equipment — twelve million dollars of revenue by year three at forty-two percent margin, for a four-point-two-million year-one investment, committed in three gates, not one leap. First: the market. This niche buys thirty to forty million a year, it is fragmented, and no incumbent serves it end-to-end. Second: the fit. Four of the five capabilities it needs already run in our plants; we add one hire. Third: the screen. Your mandate asked for ten million revenue, forty percent margin, cash-positive in three years, four to five million invested — FieldGuard passes every test, and the numbers are in your brief. The plan: validation to month six, pilot line to month eighteen, then scale. Today we ask only for gate one: six hundred thousand. If the letters of intent don't come, we stop, and the loss is capped at that. We're happy to take the board's questions."

## Exercise 4.2 — Judge-brief writing · 40 min, pairs, three rounds during autumn

Write the one-page black-and-white brief (`06-templates/judge_brief_template.docx`) for: (a) IJL's national expansion, (b) Coal & Canary's luxury move, (c) People Corp's SpringBoard trust rebuild — using the re-crack briefs in `01-competition/past_cases.md`. Compare (a)–(c) against the filled AROW model in `05-practice-cases/model_answers.md`.

**Marking checklist (score /10):** fits one page at ≥10.5 pt (2) · recommendation sentence with number and date (2) · three numbered pillars, each with a figure (2) · financial mini-table passes the case's own constraints (1) · risks named with mitigations (1) · 90-day ask (1) · survives greyscale print and a 90-second skim (1). **Instant fail:** any school identifier; wording that contradicts the summary slide.

## Exercise 4.3 — Number-attack gauntlet · 20 min, after each original-case simulation

The judge attacks only the numbers. Prepare answers for all, deliver in ≤30 seconds each, answer-first. For **case 1 (Sparkasse):** (1) "Your CLV is fantasy — defend €1,500–2,000." (2) "Why does capping interest at €5,000 halve the cost?" (3) "78% of youth accounts are parent-opened — doesn't that kill your acquisition thesis?" (4) "Your package is €7.5M — the CFO's guardrail was what, and where's the proof you're inside it?" (5) "Break-even needs 6,000–8,000 main-bank wins — your funnel produces how many, from where?" (6) "Why reject the full app rebuild?" (7) "What if neobanks match the 1.5% interest?" (8) "Which number in your deck is weakest?" For **case 2 (Lichtwerk):** the ten judge questions in its teaching note. For **case 3 (Klangfest):** (1) "Which of your options fails your own €330k floor?" (2) "Corporate production at 32% margin — why not 20?" (3) "The Westkrone exit is in four months — your plan starts when?" (4) "What does the Innovationsfonds application require and when?" (5) "Why is a taxable subsidiary not mission drift?" (6) "Your reserves last how long if year one slips?"

> **Strong-answer criteria.** States the number first, then its basis (exhibit / stated assumption / benchmark), then the sensitivity ("even at half that, the decision holds" — only if true). Saying "I don't have that number, but the bounding logic is…" scores; inventing a number fails the drill. Question 8-type traps ("your weakest number") are answered honestly + with the mitigation — never "none."

## Exercise 4.4 — Choreography drill · 10 min, before every full simulation

Run the mechanics cold: opening 20 seconds word-perfect; three handovers ("Maria will now take you through the economics" — one sentence, no "yeah so"); the yellow-card compression (each member can cut their section to half, on sight of the card); the red-card close (pre-scripted 40-word landing); clicker discipline (presenter holds it, never the talker-over); seated boardroom posture check. English fillers banned — use the pause techniques from `04-training/english_phrasebook.md` §3.

## Exercise 4.5 — Full dress rehearsal protocol · use at W17, W19, W23–24, W25

Competition-identical: printed scoresheets (`01-competition/judging_scoresheet.md`) for every judge; timed prep with USB-style hand-in; cards; anonymity language; business attire at W25. Afterwards: judges confer and rank *before* discussing with the team (mirrors real deliberation); feedback as 2 strengths / 2 gaps / 1 drill per person; scores and goals into `02-plan/simulation_log.md` the same evening; video reviewed by each member individually within 48 h with one self-selected fix reported at the next midweek session.
