# Workbook 3 — Storyline, Slides & Implementation Drills

Method reference: `04-training/presentation_and_storyline_guide.md`. Templates: `06-templates/`. The heaviest rubric category (Implementation & Resources, 2.5×) gets its own drills here — by design.

## Exercise 3.1 — Action-title rewrites · 15 min, solo, weekly until sharp

Rewrite each weak title as a full-sentence action title carrying the slide's message (invent plausible content where needed):

1. "Market Overview" 2. "Financial Analysis" 3. "Our Recommendation" 4. "Customer Segments" 5. "Risks" 6. "Digital Trends in Banking" 7. "Implementation" 8. "Competitive Landscape" 9. "Survey Results" 10. "Conclusion"

> **Model rewrites.** 1 "The €110M premium segment grows 11% — while the mid-market shrinks beneath it." 2 "The plan pays back in year 3: €2.5M buys €1.1M of annual contribution." 3 "Launch the two-tier line: protect the €6.8M core, build the margin on top." 4 "Two of four segments carry 74% of value — and both are reachable through one channel." 5 "The three real risks are priced in — and each has a trigger and an owner." 6 "Instant onboarding is table stakes: every rival opens accounts in under 10 minutes." 7 "Ninety days to first proof: pilot, gate, then scale." 8 "No incumbent serves the niche end-to-end — the window is 18–24 months." 9 "Customers grant permission to climb: 66% credit craftsmanship, 24% see luxury today." 10 "Approve €X today; the first gate reports in March." *Test: read your ten titles alone — they must tell the story.*

## Exercise 3.2 — Storyline sprint · 25 min, pairs

Take the Coal & Canary re-crack brief (`01-competition/past_cases.md` §2). Produce a title-only deck: 12 action titles in order, nothing else. Then compare against the Access CU model storyline in `05-practice-cases/model_answers.md` for shape (not content): answer-first? diagnosis before options? implementation before risks? one narrative line?

> **Marking.** A pass has: recommendation stated by title 2–3; a "what luxury requires" step; the channel-consequence step; a financial bridge; a phased roadmap title with dates; a risk title that names the biggest risk, not "risks". Common fail: ten titles of analysis, recommendation at title 11.

## Exercise 3.3 — Horizontal-logic repair · 10 min, solo

The eight titles below are shuffled. Order them into the storyline (numbers only):
A "Risks are capped: gate 1 puts only $0.6M at stake, fully sunk-cost" · B "We recommend FieldGuard: $12M by Year 3 at 42% margin, gated in three steps" · C "The screen leaves one winner from twelve candidates" · D "Growth has stalled: 90% share leaves nothing to win in transit" · E "Gate 1 asks for $0.6M — full commitment only after LOIs and prototype" · F "Four of five required capabilities already run in our plants" · G "The ag-and-construction niche buys $30–40M a year and is fragmented" · H "Three-phase roadmap: validation, pilot line, scale to $10M+"

> **Answer.** D → B → C → G → F → H → A → E (C may move ahead of B only if you argue the screen first; answer-first is non-negotiable, so B never falls below position 3). This mirrors the AROW model brief in `05-practice-cases/model_answers.md` — keep its gate figures, and note that AROW is a Canadian/US manufacturer, so every FieldGuard number is in dollars.

The Manitoba Museum returns blockbusters with a sponsor-capped downside (Ex 2.3). 4. Thiessen & Sons pilots national clienteling (mini-case 2). 5. Senne Präzisionstechnik closes 10 of 14 roles (mini-case 6).

> **Model for (3).** P1 *Now–Jun 2027* — secure title sponsor ≥$200k + book title show; owner: Development Director; cost $40k (sales + legal). P2 *Jul–Oct 2027* — marketing, schools pre-booking, dynamic pricing live; owner: Marketing Lead; $120k. P3 *Nov 2027–Feb 2028* — run show; weekly attendance vs 33.4k break-even path; owner: CEO.
