# Framework Toolkit for Case Cracking

Not a menu to recite — a toolbox. Judges punish framework name-dropping; they reward a structure tailored to the case. Use these as starting points and always adapt.

## 1. Problem framing

- **Issue tree / MECE:** break the core question into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive branches. Always start here.
- **Hypothesis-driven approach:** state a testable answer early ("We believe X because A, B, C"), then use analysis to confirm or kill it.
- **Pyramid principle (Minto):** recommendation first, then 2–4 supporting arguments, then data. Governs both the storyline and the Q&A ("answer first").

## 2. Strategy & market

- **3C:** Company – Customers – Competitors (fast situation scan).
- **Porter's Five Forces:** industry attractiveness; use selectively — only the 2–3 forces that matter for this case.
- **Value chain:** where is value created/lost; good for operations cases (cf. Jazz Winnipeg "operational idea" 2026).
- **Ansoff matrix:** growth options (market penetration / development, product development, diversification).
- **BCG matrix:** portfolio decisions.
- **SWOT:** use only as a *synthesis* slide, never as the analysis itself.

## 3. Marketing & brand

- **4P / 7P:** product, price, place, promotion (+ people, process, physical evidence for services).
- **STP:** segmentation → targeting → positioning (cf. Coal & Canary premium→luxury 2026: repositioning case).
- **Customer journey / jobs-to-be-done:** for service-innovation cases (cf. Access Credit Union & young customers 2026).
- **Brand ladder / premiumization levers:** product quality, scarcity, story, price architecture, distribution exclusivity, experience.

## 4. Finance (Team CFO owns, everyone understands)

- **Unit economics:** contribution margin per unit/customer; the single most useful quick model.
- **Break-even:** fixed costs ÷ contribution margin.
- **Market sizing:** top-down (population → filters) and bottom-up (capacity × utilization × price); sanity-check one with the other.
- **NPV / payback:** quick investment appraisal; state assumptions explicitly.
- **Sensitivity:** show best/base/worst on the one or two assumptions that matter most.
- **Financial ask:** every recommendation ends with cost, expected return, and timeline.

## 5. Innovation & tech (Innovation + CS leads own)

- **Ideation:** brainwriting 6-3-5, SCAMPER, analogies from other industries; generate 10+, present 2–3.
- **Feasibility filter:** desirability (customer wants it) – viability (makes money) – feasibility (we can build it).
- **Digital levers checklist:** automation, personalization, platform/marketplace, data monetization, AI-assisted service, channel shift.
- **MVP & pilot logic:** recommend a staged rollout with a measurable pilot; judges love de-risked implementation.

## 6. Implementation (never skip this)

- **Roadmap:** 90 days / year 1 / year 2–3 with owners and milestones.
- **Risks & mitigations table:** 3–4 real risks, one line of mitigation each.
- **KPIs:** how the client will know it worked.

## 7. The standard storyline skeleton

1. Situation & core question (1 slide)
2. Recommendation up front (1 slide — the money slide)
3. Why: argument 1 / 2 / 3 with evidence
4. Financials: cost, return, sensitivity
5. Implementation roadmap
6. Risks & mitigations
7. The ask / vision close
8. Appendix bank for Q&A

## 8. Q&A protocol

- Answer first, then explain. Max 60 seconds per answer.
- Bridge, don't block: "We focused on X; directionally, Y would mean…"
- Q&A quarterback routes questions to the best-placed teammate.
- Never contradict a teammate in front of judges; add, don't correct.
